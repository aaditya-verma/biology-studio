## The question (D3)
"Two flat sheets of cells. One week later, three. How does that happen, and where does each new layer come from?" Answered at the close on the same section view: *cells dive in through the streak; the first replace the hypoblast as endoderm, the next become mesoderm, those left on top become ectoderm — all three from the epiblast.*

## Objectives (H1 — one per beat)
1. **The groove:** describe where the primitive streak forms, its node and the axis it sets (E05.01.02).
2. **The dive:** explain how epiblast cells move through the streak to form endoderm, then mesoderm, leaving ectoderm (E05.01.03, E05.01.01).
3. **The section:** name the three layers in dorsal → ventral order on a transverse section (E00.01.04) and say they all come from the epiblast (E05.01.04).
4. **The rod (MBBS):** describe how the notochord forms in the midline and what is left of it in the adult (E05.02.01, book answer, D-029).
5. **Clinical link (MBBS):** state that the streak disappears after week 4 and that a remnant can form a sacrococcygeal teratoma (E05.01.02).

## Story spine (ABT, playbook §5)
**And** a flat disc of two layers sits ready in week three. **But** a body needs three layers — where can a third come from? **Therefore** cells from the top layer dive in through a groove and rebuild the layers below. Protagonist: one marked epiblast cell (a metaphor-free "we follow it" — cells don't want, D5). Stakes (MBBS): leftover streak cells can form a teratoma. Payoff: the section view, our cell in the middle layer. Motif for the season: the section view and the day stamp.

## Beats (one idea each; "this becomes…" D4; section view D9)
1. **Open** — host question → cut to the two-layer disc → terms strip (5 s, method 2).
2. **The groove** (sc-E00.01.04, day 15 → 16) — streak, the mound at its head end ("keep an eye on it"; named at beat 4), the long axis it marks.
3. **The dive** (sc-E00.01.04, "back to day fifteen" → 17, lengthwise cut) — the marked cell goes in (invagination); first cells replace the hypoblast; ours becomes mesoderm and spreads; cells left on top become ectoderm → **transverse section** (D9).
4. **The rod** (sc-E05.02.01, "back to day sixteen" → 18; MBBS; opens on our cell out in the mesoderm) — node → midline → plate in the endoderm → rod; book order; ⚑ pointer; **this becomes** the nucleus pulposus; mesoderm becomes muscle, bone, heart, vessels, blood.
5. **Clinical link** (MBBS, 15–30 s) — "here, at day 17, the groove our cell went through is still adding cells"; it shrinks after week 4; remnant → sacrococcygeal teratoma (our diagram, "Week 4 · schematic").
6. **Close** — back to the section with our cell ringed in the mesoderm, answer, 2 Checks + reveals, teaser ≤ 5 s.
