## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E13.00.01 The heart works first · AN25.2, AN79.4
- **The heart is the first organ to work.** It starts beating at **about day 21**, while it is still a simple tube — long before it has chambers or valves. `[E13.07, E13.09]` **✔** (book only; no human imaging source for the first beat retrieved — §10)
- **Why so early:** in the middle of week 3 the embryo is too big to feed itself by diffusion, so a pump and vessels appear together. `[E13.01]` **✔**
- **At first every heart muscle cell can set the pace;** the pacemaker then moves to the caudal left part of the tube, then to the sinus venosus, which ends up as the **sinuatrial node** near the superior vena cava. `[E13.07]` **✔**

### E13.02.01 Heart tube forms · AN25.2, AN22.1
- **Head folding carries the heart down.** The heart field starts cranial to the oropharyngeal membrane; the fast-growing brain folds over it and swings it first into the neck, then into the chest. `[E13.02]` **✔**
- **Side folding brings the two limbs of the crescent together**; they **fuse only at the caudal end** — the outflow tract and most of the ventricle form by the crescent's middle part **expanding**, not by two tubes zipping up. Result by day 22: one slightly bent tube inside the pericardial cavity. `[E13.02, E13.09 · R05en]` **✔✔** for a single tube by the end of week 4 (R05en, *human*, by stage) · **✔** for the caudal-fusion / expansion pattern (book only — R05en's abstract does not describe it)
- **Three layers:** endocardium (inside), myocardium (wall), epicardium (outside) — with **cardiac jelly** (hyaluronan-rich matrix) between endocardium and myocardium. The epicardium grows over from the **proepicardial organ** at the caudal edge of the dorsal mesocardium and later makes the **coronary arteries'** endothelium and smooth muscle. `[E13.02, E13.08]` **✔**
- **The tube hangs from a dorsal mesocardium only — there is never a ventral one.** Its middle breaks down, leaving the **transverse pericardial sinus**; the heart is then held only by its vessels at each end (the surgical finger-through-the-sinus space of AN22.1). `[E13.02]` **✔**

### E13.02.02 Parts of the heart tube · AN25.2, AN22.2
- **Two fields build different parts.** The **primary heart field** gives the **left ventricle and parts of both atria**; the **secondary heart field** (splanchnic mesoderm under the pharynx) adds cells at both poles: the **right ventricle and outflow tract** at the arterial end, the rest of the atria and the sinus venosus at the venous end. `[E13.01, E13.09]` **✔** (field lineages come from *mouse*/*chick*; R05en records the human outlet developing later than the inlet — ✔✔ for that order only)
- **Along the looped tube, from inflow to outflow:** sinus venosus → common atrium → AV canal → primitive (left) ventricle → bulbus cordis (its proximal third = trabeculated right ventricle, middle = **conus cordis** → both outflow tracts, distal = **truncus arteriosus** → roots of aorta and pulmonary trunk) → aortic sac. `[E13.03]` **✔**
- **Adult anchor (D4):** the ventricle–bulbus junction is the **primary interventricular foramen**; the trabeculated areas each side of it become the ventricles' rough walls, the bulbus stays smooth for a while — the smooth outflow parts of the adult ventricles (conus) come from it. `[E13.03]` **✔**

### E13.03.01 Cardiac looping · AN25.2
- **Looping starts on day 23 and is complete by day 28.** As the outflow end lengthens (cells added from the SHF), the cranial part of the tube swings **forward, downward and rightward** (ventral, caudal, right), the atrial part shifts **dorsally, cranially and to the left**. `[E13.03 · R05en]` **✔✔** by stage (R05en *human*: looping within CS9–14; on the atlas's revised scale "week 5" — D-030)
- **What looping achieves:** atria end up behind and above, ventricles in front and below, with the future right ventricle on the right — the adult layout, before any septum exists. `[E13.03, E13.09]` **✔**
- **The direction is set by left–right signalling** (Nodal → **PITX2** on the left side, programmed in the heart fields during days 16–18). Loop the wrong way and the heart ends up on the right (**dextrocardia**, E13.03.02). `[E13.01 CC, E13.03 CC, E13.04]` **✔**
- **Why the SHF matters for looping:** if the outflow tract cannot lengthen, looping fails and the outflow tract is too short — DORV, VSD, tetralogy, pulmonary atresia and stenosis follow (Tier C, §4 group 2). `[E13.02]` **✔**

### E13.06.01 How septa form · AN25.2
- **Two ways to build a wall.** (1) **Endocardial cushions** — the matrix under the endocardium swells, cells move in (from endocardium in the AV canal, from **neural crest** in the outflow tract), and opposing cushions grow until they fuse; one cushion can also grow across alone. (2) **Uneven growth** — a strip of wall stays still while both sides balloon, leaving a ridge that never divides completely; the gap is plugged later by neighbouring tissue (muscular ventricular septum, septum secundum). `[E13.06]` **✔**
- **When:** the main septa form **between days 27 and 37**, as the embryo grows from about 5 to 16–17 mm. `[E13.06]` **✔✔** by stage (R05en *human*: compartments in week 6 and septation in weeks 7–8 on its scale; venous-pole septation complete by **CS17**, interventricular closure by **CS20**)
- **Why cushions matter clinically:** the same cushion tissue helps make the atrial septum, the membranous ventricular septum, both AV valves and the outflow septum — so one cushion problem can show up as ASD, VSD and great-vessel defects together. `[E13.06]` **✔**

### E13.06.02 Atrial septation · AN25.2, AN22.2
- **Septum primum** grows down from the roof at the end of week 4 towards the AV cushions; the gap below its edge is the **ostium primum**. Before cushion tissue closes that gap, **programmed cell death** punches holes high up that join into the **ostium secundum** — so there is always a way for blood to cross. `[E13.06]` **✔**
- **Septum secundum** then forms to the right of the primum as a **thick, incomplete crescent**; its free edge overlaps the ostium secundum. The gap it leaves is the **foramen ovale**, and the remaining septum primum becomes the **flap (valve) of the foramen ovale**. Blood passes through an oblique slit from right to left. `[E13.06 · R05mj]` **✔** · ✚ wording: Anderson's group labels septum secundum the **infolded atrial roof** (R05mj, in passing, 102 *human* hypoplastic-left-heart specimens) — it matches the book's "fold"; no conflict
- **The foramen ovale is a flap valve, not a hole:** high right-atrial pressure in the fetus pushes the flap open; after birth left-atrial pressure presses it shut (functional closure), and fusion follows over about **a year**. `[E13.06, E13.08]` **✔**
- **Probe patency:** the book says fusion stays incomplete in **about 20 %**, leaving a slit that does not normally shunt. A Tier 1 autopsy study of 965 normal hearts finds **27.3 %** overall, falling with age (34 % in the first three decades → 20 % in the ninth and tenth). `[E13.06, E13.08 · R05es]` **⚑ D-068 b**
- **Left atrium:** the pulmonary vein forms **in the dorsal mesocardium** (dorsal mesenchymal protrusion) and is carried into the left atrium's back wall as the septum primum grows down; the atrium then absorbs its stem until **four** veins open separately. Each atrium = its own trabeculated **appendage** (the embryonic atrium) + a smooth part from absorbed vessel (sinus venosus on the right, pulmonary vein on the left). `[E13.06]` **✔**

### E13.06.05 Outflow tract septation · AN25.2
- In week 5 two **truncus swellings** (right superior, left inferior) appear and grow towards the aortic sac while **twisting around each other**; fused, they make the **spiral aorticopulmonary septum**. Matching **conus swellings** fuse with it and split the conus into the right ventricle's outflow (anterolateral) and the left's (posteromedial). `[E13.06]` **✔✔** (R05en *human*: outflow remodelling at CS17–18 moves the spiral course into the arterial trunks)
- **Cardiac neural crest** from the hindbrain folds migrates through pharyngeal arches **3, 4 and 6** into the outflow cushions; the SHF regulates it through **NOTCH–JAG1**. Crest also builds the **face**, which is why outflow and facial defects travel together (DiGeorge). `[E13.04, E13.06]` **✔** (mechanism from *chick*/*mouse*; the human link is the 22q11 clinical data, §4)
- **The spiral is why the great arteries twist:** it lets the aorta leave the left ventricle and the pulmonary trunk the right; the book ties the spiral to SHF laterality signalling. A straight septum = **transposition** (§4 group 4). `[E13.01, E13.06]` **✔**
- **Semilunar valves** start as three tubercles per channel on the truncus swellings and hollow out on their upper surface; neural crest contributes. `[E13.06]` **✔**

### E13.08.01 Pharyngeal arch arteries and their fates · AN25.6, AN23.4
- Each pharyngeal arch gets its own artery from the **aortic sac**, running to the paired dorsal aortae. They appear **one after another, cranial to caudal, and are never all present at once**; the 5th either never forms or forms partly and regresses, so they are numbered 1, 2, 3, 4, 6. `[E13.08]` **✔** · ✚ a true transient 5th-arch artery was seen in **one human CS14 embryo** (R05ex)
- **Fates, grouped by adult destination:** *The aortic arch and its branches* — the aortic sac's left horn gives the first part of the arch and its right horn the brachiocephalic artery; the **left 4th** artery makes the arch between left common carotid and left subclavian; the **right 4th** makes only the start of the right subclavian (the rest from right dorsal aorta + 7th intersegmental). *The lungs and the fetal bypass* — the **6th** pair: proximal parts → pulmonary arteries; on the left the distal part stays as the **ductus arteriosus**, on the right it is lost. *The head and neck* — the **3rd** → common carotid and the start of the internal carotid (the rest of the internal carotid is dorsal aorta; the external carotid sprouts from the 3rd); the 1st and 2nd mostly vanish, leaving small remnants (maxillary; hyoid and stapedial). `[E13.08]` **✔** · **⚑ D-068 a** where the pulmonary arteries really come from
- **Neural crest** gives the arch arteries' smooth muscle and patterns them; **PITX2** sets the left–right asymmetry. `[E13.08]` **✔** · ✚ arch-artery endothelium carries the crest marker AP2α in *human* embryos (R05ew)
- **Why the heart's descent matters:** as it drops into the chest the carotid duct (dorsal aorta between arches 3 and 4) and the right dorsal aorta below the 7th intersegmental artery disappear, the left subclavian's origin climbs to near the left common carotid, and the **recurrent laryngeal nerves** are left hooked under the 6th-arch remnants (E13.08.02). `[E13.08]` **✔**

### E13.08.06 Fetal circulation · AN25.3, AN5.2
- **The placenta, not the lungs, oxygenates the blood.** Umbilical-vein blood is about **80 % saturated**; most of it skips the liver through the **ductus venosus** into the IVC (a sphincter can limit inflow when contractions push venous return too high). `[E13.08 · R05mh, R05mi]` **✔** the bypass · **⚑ D-091 b** the sphincter (R05mh, *human* histology + SEM of 28 fetuses at 13–17 weeks of gestation: an **elastin-rich shelf** at the inlet and a narrow isthmus, **no smooth-muscle sphincter**; R05mi, *human* ultrasound of 2 growth-restricted fetuses: the duct's width changed by more than 80 % during a 45-min scan — flow is regulated, but no muscle ring has been shown)
- **Three shunts, three jobs:** *ductus venosus* — liver bypass; *foramen ovale* — the IVC's richer blood is steered by the **valve of the IVC** across to the left atrium, the lower edge of septum secundum (**crista dividens**) holding some back; *ductus arteriosus* — because fetal lung resistance is high, most right-ventricle (SVC) blood goes from pulmonary trunk to descending aorta. `[E13.08]` **✔**
- **Best blood to heart and brain:** left-ventricle blood goes first to the coronary and carotid arteries; the mix in the descending aorta is poorer and returns by the two umbilical arteries at about **58 %**. Mixing happens at five places: liver, IVC, right atrium, left atrium, ductus entry. `[E13.08]` **✔** (saturations are book-only — no Tier 1 retrieved, §10)

### E13.08.07 Changes at birth and adult remnants · AN25.3
- **Two triggers:** placental flow stops and breathing begins. Lung flow rises, **left-atrial pressure rises**, right-atrial pressure falls → the flap meets septum secundum and the **foramen ovale closes functionally**. `[E13.08]` **✔**
- **Closure order and timing:** umbilical arteries contract within **minutes** (anatomical lumen loss 2–3 months); umbilical vein and ductus venosus shortly after (so placental blood can still reach the baby for a while); **ductus arteriosus** contracts almost at once, triggered by bradykinin from the inflating lungs, anatomical closure **1–3 months**. `[E13.08 · R05et]` **✔✔** for the onset of ductal narrowing within hours (R05et, *human* echo: a local intraluminal protrusion, seen in all 50 term newborns by 24 h) · **✔** for bradykinin and the 1–3-month anatomical closure (book only)
- **Early closure is reversible:** in the first days a crying baby can push blood right-to-left through the flap — the cause of brief **cyanotic spells** in newborns. Fusion takes about a year; a probe-patent slit remains in some (⚑ D-068 b). `[E13.08]` **✔**
- **Adult remnants:** ductus arteriosus → **ligamentum arteriosum**; umbilical vein → **ligamentum teres hepatis**; ductus venosus → **ligamentum venosum**; umbilical arteries → **medial umbilical ligaments** (distal) and **superior vesical arteries** (proximal); foramen ovale → **fossa ovalis**. `[E13.08, E13.09]` **✔**

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"The heart starts beating once it has four chambers."** (map row) It beats from **about day 21**, as a tube, weeks before any septum `[E13.07]` ✔.
2. **"Two heart tubes fuse along their whole length."** They fuse only at the **caudal** end; the outflow tract and most of the ventricle come from the crescent **expanding** `[E13.02]` ✔.
3. **"The heart forms in the chest."** It forms **in front of the head**, cranial to the oropharyngeal membrane, and head folding swings it down through the neck `[E13.02]` ✔.
4. **"Looping is just the tube bending to fit."** Looping has a **fixed direction (rightward)** set by left–right signalling; bend the other way and you get dextrocardia `[E13.03, E13.01 CC]` ✔.
5. **"Dextrocardia always means every organ is reversed."** With **situs inversus** it does; in **heterotaxy** only some organs are reversed, and heart defects are common `[E13.03 CC]` ✔.
6. **"The foramen ovale is a hole that should have closed."** It is a **flap valve** that must stay open before birth; closing it early is a lethal defect `[E13.06, E13.06 CC]` ✔.
7. **"The foramen ovale closes before birth."** (map row) It closes **functionally at birth** and fuses over about **a year**; about 1 in 5 adults keep a probe-patent slit (book) · ⚑ newer autopsy data ~1 in 4 (D-068 b) `[E13.08 · R05es]` (*Checks give "after birth", not the percentage, until decided*).
8. **"Ductus arteriosus and ductus venosus do the same job."** *Arteriosus*: pulmonary trunk → aorta, **lung** bypass, → ligamentum arteriosum. *Venosus*: umbilical vein → IVC, **liver** bypass, → ligamentum venosum `[E13.08]` ✔.
9. **"The fetal lungs oxygenate the blood."** (map row) The **placenta** does; fetal lungs are high-resistance and mostly bypassed `[E13.08]` ✔.
10. **"Umbilical veins carry deoxygenated blood, like other veins."** The umbilical **vein** carries the best blood (~80 % saturated); the umbilical **arteries** carry the poorest (~58 %) — vessels are named by direction, not oxygen `[E13.08]` ✔.
11. **"A 'blue baby' has a hole in the heart."** Blueness (cyanosis) means **deoxygenated blood reaching the body**: a **right-to-left** shunt (tetralogy's override, a crying newborn's flap) or **parallel** circuits (TGA). A plain VSD, ASD or PDA shunts **left-to-right** and is not blue at first `[E13.06 CC, E13.08]` ✔.
12. **"Tetralogy is four separate defects."** It is **one** misplaced (anterior) conotruncal septum; the RV hypertrophy is a **consequence** `[E13.06 CC]` ✔.
13. **"In transposition the septum twists the wrong way."** It **does not spiral at all** — it runs straight down `[E13.06 CC]` ✔.
14. **"All six arch arteries are present at the same time."** (map row) They appear and disappear **in sequence**, and the 5th rarely forms `[E13.08 · R05ex]` ✔ · ✚.
15. **"The two recurrent laryngeal nerves loop round the same artery."** Right → **right subclavian**; left → **ligamentum arteriosum** — because different arch segments survive `[E13.08]` ✔.
16. **"The pulmonary veins grow out of the left atrium."** They form in the **dorsal mesocardium** and are carried in by the DMP; that is why misplacing the DMP gives TAPVR `[E13.06, E13.06 CC]` ✔.
17. **"Septum secundum is a complete second wall."** It is **always incomplete** — its gap is the foramen ovale `[E13.06]` ✔.
18. **"Coarctation raises blood pressure everywhere."** Arms high, **legs low** — the narrowing sits beyond the arm vessels `[E13.08 CC]` ✔.
19. **"The conducting system is nerve tissue."** It is **modified heart muscle** (TBX3 keeps it from becoming working muscle); nerves only adjust the rate `[E13.07]` ✔.
20. **"Most heart defects are inherited."** Most have no single known cause — the book puts most down to multifactorial causes (12 % chromosomal, ~2 % environmental) `[E13.06 CC]` ✔ · ✚ about 1 in 5 has a known cause (R05ep, citing earlier work in its introduction).

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
- **Three moves on one tube:** fold (into the chest), loop (to the right), divide (four rooms + two arteries). Teach them as one continuous animation, then freeze-frame each.
- **Holes that are supposed to be there.** Ostium primum, ostium secundum, foramen ovale, interventricular foramen, ductus — learners file every opening as a defect. Sort them early: *normal and temporary* vs *defect when it persists*.
- **Names change as parts move:** bulbus → conus + truncus → outflow tracts; sinus horn → sinus venarum / coronary sinus. One labelled timeline beats prose.
- **Left–right asymmetry everywhere:** looping, arch fates, recurrent nerves, caval shunts — one cause (laterality signalling, left-to-right venous shunting) explains many "why is the left different?" questions. Name it once, reuse it.
- **Shunt direction decides symptoms.** Pressure, not hole size, decides which way blood goes (fetal right-high, adult left-high). Teach pressure first, then the defects.
