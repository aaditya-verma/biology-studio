## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E14.01.01 Respiratory diverticulum · AN25.2, AN79.4
- **The lungs grow out of the gut.** At about 4 weeks a pouch buds from the **front (ventral) wall of the foregut**, just below the pharynx. Everything that lines the airway — larynx, trachea, bronchi, alveoli — is **endoderm**, the same layer that lines the gut. `[E14.01, E14.05 · R05fb]` **✔✔** (bud at CS12 in human specimens)
- **Two layers, two jobs.** The endoderm makes the lining; the **splanchnic (visceral) mesoderm** wrapped round the foregut makes the cartilage rings, smooth muscle and connective tissue. `[E14.01, E14.05]` **✔**
- **Why there:** mesoderm beside the bud site raises **retinoic acid**, which switches on **TBX4** in the gut endoderm; TBX4 starts the bud and keeps the lungs growing and differentiating. `[E14.01]` **✔** (signal order from *mouse*) · **✔✔ that TBX4 matters in humans:** deletions or variants of **TBX4 (or its partner FGF10)** were found in 16 of 26 infants who died of lethal lung hypoplasia (R05fo, *human*). `[E14.01 · R05fo]`
- **The tube stays open at the top.** The new respiratory tube keeps its connection with the pharynx through the **laryngeal opening** — the future glottis. `[E14.01]` **✔**
- **How the two tubes part (⚑ D-072 a).** Book: two **tracheo-oesophageal ridges** grow in from the sides and **fuse into a septum**, splitting the foregut into oesophagus behind and trachea in front; this is done in week 4. `[E14.01, E14.05]` **✔** for the result and timing · **⚑** for the mechanism: human staged embryos show **no zip-up from below** — the two tubes take separate courses as soon as the bud appears, a mesenchymal septum comes between them, and the separation point stays at one level while the carina descends (R05fc); the saddle-shaped sulcus at the top of the split stays at the level of C1 while both tubes lengthen (R05fb); in *mouse* and *frog* a short-lived epithelial septum does form by medial pinching and fusion, then remodels (R05fe). **Tier 1 is split:** a human-centred review describes separation running from the lung buds upwards (R05ff), as the book implies. Card + Check use the book answer until the owner picks.

### E14.04.01 Lung maturation phases · AN25.2, AN25.1
- **Four overlapping phases, named after what the tissue looks like.** **Pseudoglandular** (weeks 5–16: looks like a gland — branching tubes in mesenchyme, no gas exchange), **canalicular** (16–26: the tubes widen into canals and capillaries move in), **terminal sac / saccular** (26 to birth: thin sacs, capillaries pressed against them), **alveolar** (late fetal life into childhood: true alveoli). `[E14.04, E14.05 · R05ff]` **✔✔** (R05ff uses the same names and nearly the same weeks after conception: 5–17, 16–26, 24–38, alveolarization from ~36 wk) — *the phases overlap; edges are soft, not dates to memorize to the week*.
- **Gas exchange needs two cell types and a thin wall.** Cells lining the sacs flatten into **type I** cells, so capillaries bulge into the air space — the **blood–air barrier**. **Type II** cells appear late in month 6 and make **surfactant**, a phospholipid-rich film that lowers surface tension. `[E14.04, E14.05 · R05ff]` **✔✔** (R05ff: first air–blood barriers and surfactant in the canalicular stage; small amounts of surfactant at 22–24 wk after conception)
- **The fetal lung is full of liquid, not empty.** The lung makes its own liquid by pumping chloride into the airways; the book notes it is chloride-rich and protein-poor, and that its surfactant content climbs most steeply just before birth. `[E14.04 · R05fv]` **✔✔** (R05fv: net liquid secretion driven by active chloride transport; its volume is vital for normal lung growth)
- **The fetus practises breathing.** **Fetal breathing movements** start well before birth, draw amniotic fluid in, and help the lung grow and the breathing muscles train. `[E14.04 · R05ml]` **✔✔** start well before birth · **✔** fluid intake, lung growth, muscle training (book) (R05ml, *human* longitudinal ultrasound of 12 pregnancies: breathing movements are already present by **10 weeks of gestation** (menstrual clock ≈ 8 weeks after fertilization) and grow more frequent and faster through 19 weeks — the exact first appearance is in part I of the series, which has no abstract, §10)
- **At birth the liquid goes, the surfactant stays.** Most lung liquid is absorbed into blood and lymph capillaries (a little is squeezed out during delivery); the surfactant film left on the alveolar walls stops them **collapsing on breathing out** (atelectasis). `[E14.04 · R05fv]` **✔✔** (R05fv: towards term the epithelial sodium channel ENaC rises; fetal adrenaline in labour switches the lung from secreting to absorbing — *sheep*-led data, mechanism only)
- **After birth the lung grows mostly by adding alveoli, not by inflating them.** Book: about **one-sixth** of the adult number at birth, the rest added over the **first 10 years**. `[E14.04, E14.05]` **✔** for "more, not bigger" · **⚑** for the numbers (D-072 b): human counts give **~150 million at birth**, which R05fl itself calls about **half** the adult number; against the stereological adult mean of **~480 million** (R05fm) it is about **one-third** — either way far more than one-sixth — and new alveoli are still added between 7 and 21 years (R05fn, *human* ³He MRI: ~1.9-fold more across that span); R05ff dates continued alveolarization to young adulthood.
- **Alveoli begin shortly before birth.** True alveoli start forming in late fetal life — in some fetuses at 32 weeks, in all by 36 weeks of gestation (R05fk). Book: no mature alveoli exist until after birth; its alveolar phase starts at month 8. `[E14.04 · R05fk, R05ff]` **✔✔** — read together, the book means the walls are not yet mature (R05ff: septa still carry a double capillary layer at birth); cards say "alveoli begin to form shortly before birth" (§5, not a ⚑).

**Group 1 — the tubes didn't part (E14.01.02, AN25.4).**
- **What it is:** faulty separation of trachea and oesophagus → **oesophageal atresia** (a blind-ending oesophagus) with or without a **tracheo-oesophageal fistula** (an abnormal channel between them). The common form (≈ 9 in 10): blind upper pouch, lower oesophagus joined to the trachea. `[E14.01 CC · R05fq]` **✔✔**
- **How it shows:** the fetus can't swallow amniotic fluid down to the gut → **polyhydramnios**; at birth the baby is **frothy and drooly** and a feeding tube stops short; swallowed fluid or stomach contents can reach the lungs through the fistula → **pneumonitis**. `[E14.01 CC · R05fq]` **✔✔** (R05fq: a tube passed at birth after polyhydramnios stops ~10 cm from the mouth; polyhydramnios alone is a weak sign — ~1 % have EA)
- **It rarely comes alone:** heart defects in about a third; part of **VACTERL** (vertebral, anal, cardiac, tracheo-oesophageal, renal, limb) — defects found together more often than chance, cause unknown. `[E14.01 CC · R05fq, R05fp]` **✔✔**
- **Molecular depth (Tier D, beyond Langman):** in *mouse* and *frog*, Hedgehog–Gli signalling acts through **Foxf1** splanchnic mesenchyme to pinch the foregut in at the border between **SOX2** (future oesophagus) and **NKX2-1** (future trachea) epithelium; a boundary epithelium carrying both markers fuses into a short-lived septum that is then remodelled — Gli-deficient embryos fail to separate (R05fe). `[E14.01 · R05fe]` **✚**

**Group 2 — the tree budded or branched wrongly (E14.04.03)** — ordered by how much they matter to the child.
- **Cysts that get infected (most important clinically).** When airways balloon out during development, the child is left with fluid-filled pockets — one or two big ones, or many small ones that look like a honeycomb on a chest film; because they empty badly, infection keeps returning. Modern names: congenital lung malformations / CPAM; antenatally detected cases rose more than 3-fold in one UK register (R05fw). `[E14.04 CC · R05fw]` **✔** · ✚
- **An extra bud of its own.** A second respiratory bud from the trachea or oesophagus can grow into an **ectopic lobe** separate from the main tree; the version clinicians meet most is a **tracheal bronchus** to the right upper lobe, often with heart defects. `[E14.04 CC · R05fx]` **✔** · ✚ (R05fx)
- **Harmless extra branches.** Unusual branching (extra lobules) is common and rarely matters — except as a surprise during bronchoscopy. `[E14.04 CC]` **✔**
- **No lung at all.** Agenesis (one lung, or a blind trachea with none) is described but **rare**. `[E14.04 CC]` **✔** (no rate — §10)

**Group 3 — the lung didn't grow (hypoplasia).**
- The book names hypoplasia only in passing here; its main cause in this course is **CDH** (Ch 7): gut in the chest squeezes the lung (D-043). ✚ depth: in a *rat* nitrofen model the lung is already under-branched before the diaphragm defect could compress it — the **dual-hit** idea (R05fy). `[E07.05 CC · R05ab, R05fy]` ✔ · ✚
- **Genes can do it alone:** TBX4/FGF10 deletions or variants (17q23.1q23.2) in lethal **acinar dysplasia** and other primary hypoplasias (R05fo, *human*). `[E14.01, E14.03 · R05fo]` **✚** (supports the book's TBX4 and FGF lines; see D-067 note in §5)

**Group 4 — the lung didn't mature (RDS).**
- **RDS = too little surfactant** in a preterm lung; alveoli collapse on breathing out. The book calls it a common cause of preterm death, ~20 % of newborn deaths. `[E14.04 CC]` **✔** (death share not checked) · **✔✔** for "common" only: RDS in **93 %** of 9,575 infants born at 22–28 weeks of gestation (R05fs) — a morbidity rate.
- **Steroids before birth work:** a single course for women at risk of preterm birth cuts RDS (RR 0.71), neonatal death (RR 0.78) and perinatal death (RR 0.85), high-certainty evidence, in rich and poor settings alike (R05fr). Surfactant for the baby helps too: prophylactic synthetic surfactant cut neonatal deaths (RR 0.70) and air leaks (R05fz). `[E14.04 CC · R05fr, R05fz]` **✔✔** (no dosing on cards — D6)
- **Who survives (⚑ D-072 c):** book — gas exchange adequate and survival possible by the **end of month 7** (≈ 28–30 weeks after fertilization; its month length is undefined); cohorts: survival to discharge **6 % at 22 weeks and 92 % at 28 weeks** (NICHD 2003–07, R05fs), **33 % at 22 weeks** among infants actively resuscitated (California 2011–19, R05ft) — weeks of gestational age, ≈ 20 weeks after fertilization.

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decision D-072 (owner picks)
- **(a) How trachea and oesophagus separate.** Book: lateral ridges meet and fuse into a septum (week 4). Tier 1 is **split**. Against the book: human staged embryos (R05fc) — no common chamber, **no caudal-to-cranial separation**, a mesenchymal septum between two tubes whose separation point stays at one level while the carina descends; the top of the split (a saddle-shaped sulcus) stays at C1 while both tubes lengthen (R05fb). ## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"The lungs come from the chest wall / from mesoderm."** (map row) The lining of every airway is **gut endoderm** — the lungs bud off the foregut; mesoderm only wraps it `[E14.01 · R05fb]` ✔✔.
2. **"The lung bud grows from the back of the gut."** It grows from the **front (ventral)** wall — which is why the trachea lies in front of the oesophagus `[E14.01]` ✔.
3. **"The trachea and oesophagus are one tube until late, then split."** (map row) They are separated early — within weeks 4–6 (CS13–16) — and a septum ends up between them; *how* it forms is debated `[E14.01 · R05fd, R05fc]` ✔✔ timing · ⚑ D-072 a (*Checks ask what separates them and when, never the direction, until decided*).
4. **"A fistula means a hole in the oesophagus."** It is a channel **between** trachea and oesophagus; atresia is the **blind end** — most babies have both `[E14.01 CC]` ✔.
5. **"Polyhydramnios means the baby is making too much fluid."** Here it means the fetus **can't swallow it away** — fluid normally passes down the gut `[E14.01 CC · R05fq]` ✔✔.
6. **"The larynx is just the top of the trachea."** Its lining is endoderm like the trachea, but its **cartilages and muscles come from the surrounding arch mesoderm** (R05fj) — in the book arches 4 and 6, which is why it is wired by **vagal branches**, superior (4) and recurrent (6) `[E14.02 · R05fj]` ✔✔ mesoderm origin · ✔ arch numbers + nerves (book).
7. **"Left and right lungs are built the same."** Right: 3 lobar, 10 segmental bronchi; left: 2 and 8 — set in week 5–8 `[E14.03 · R05fh]` ✔✔.
8. **"The fetal lungs are collapsed and empty."** (map row) They are **full of liquid the lung secretes itself**, and that liquid is needed for normal growth `[E14.04 · R05fv]` ✔✔.
9. **"The first breath is the first breathing movement."** (map row) The fetus makes **breathing movements** long before birth, moving amniotic fluid; they help the lung grow `[E14.04]` ✔.
10. **"The first breath inflates the lung by pushing the liquid out of the mouth."** Most of it is **absorbed** into blood and lymph capillaries; only a little leaves through the airway `[E14.04 · R05fv]` ✔✔.
11. **"Preterm babies struggle because their lungs are too small."** (map row) The decisive lack is **surfactant** and thin gas-exchange walls; too little surfactant → alveoli collapse on each breath out (RDS) `[E14.04, E14.04 CC · R05ff]` ✔✔ (R05ff: early preterm survival depends mostly on available surfactant).
12. **"Surfactant is just a protein that helps oxygen across."** It is **mostly phospholipid** (with some proteins such as SP-A), and its job is to **lower surface tension**, not to carry gas `[E14.04 · R05fu]` ✔.
13. **"Steroids before birth are given to the baby."** They are given to the **mother** to speed the fetal lungs `[E14.04 CC · R05fr]` ✔✔.
14. **"All alveoli are formed before birth."** (map row) Only a fraction exist at birth; most are added after — book says through childhood, newer data into adolescence `[E14.04 · R05fl, R05fn]` ✔ · ⚑ D-072 b (*Checks ask "more alveoli or bigger alveoli?", not the count*).
15. **"The lung grows after birth only by its alveoli getting bigger."** It grows mainly by making **more** alveoli (book); R05fn confirms new alveoli keep forming through adolescence, alongside some enlargement `[E14.04, E14.05 · R05fn]` ✔ "mainly" (book) · ✔✔ new alveoli after birth.
16. **"The phases happen one after another, all at once in the whole lung."** (More / Tier D, beyond Langman) They **overlap** and run from the centre out — central airways mature first `[— · R05ff]` ✚.
17. **"Pleura is a separate sheet that wraps the lung later."** The **visceral** layer is the lung's own covering of splanchnic mesoderm; the **parietal** layer is the body wall's somatic mesoderm `[E14.03 · R05ff]` ✔✔.

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
- **Two germ layers in one organ:** "endoderm lining, mesoderm wall" — say it once for the trachea and reuse it for larynx, bronchi, pleura.
- **Phase names describe looks, not jobs.** Teach each with one picture and one question ("can it exchange gas yet?") — only from canalicular on.
- **Three clocks** (weeks after fertilization, gestational weeks, months): always print the clock beside the number; viability is the trap (D-072 c).
- **Arch numbers come back:** larynx = arches 4 + 6 = the same recurrent-nerve story as Ch 13's arch arteries. Link, don't re-teach.
- **The lung is not finished at birth.** Learners assume birth = done; surfactant, alveoli and growth all keep going.
