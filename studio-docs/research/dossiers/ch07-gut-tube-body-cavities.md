## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E07.01.01 Body plan after folding · AN52.6
- In weeks 3–4 the ectoderm rolls up into the neural tube (dorsal) and, almost at the same time, the endoderm rolls into the gut tube (ventral): a tube on top of a tube, held together by mesoderm. `[E07.01, E07.06 · R06 CS10–12]` **✔** (R06 confirms each process at these stages; the "tube on a tube" picture is the book's)
- The lateral body-wall folds (parietal mesoderm + ectoderm + cells from nearby somites) swing ventrally and meet in the midline, closing the ventral body wall by the end of week 4; head and tail folds help by curling the embryo. `[E07.01, E07.02]` **✔** (later muscle/rib layer: see ⚑ D-043 a)
- Closure stays open at one place, the connecting stalk (future umbilical cord); the gut stays open to the yolk sac there through the vitelline duct. `[E07.01 · R06 CS12–13]` **✔✔**
- The vitelline duct joins the cord, thins and disappears with the yolk sac in months 2–3. `[E07.01]` **✔**
- Where the gut meets the back wall, the parietal and visceral layers stay continuous with each other. `[E07.01]` **✔**

### E07.02.01 Intraembryonic body cavity · AN79.4
- At the end of week 3 intraembryonic mesoderm forms three strips; the lateral plate is the one that builds the body cavity. `[E07.02, E06.02]` **✔**
- Clefts appear in the solid lateral plate and merge, splitting it into two layers; the gap between them is the primitive body cavity. `[E07.02 · R06 CS9]` **✔✔** (R06: vesicles fuse; one CS9 embryo still has separate left and right cavities)
- At first it is one continuous cavity — not yet split into pericardial, pleural and abdominopelvic parts. `[E07.01]` **✔**
- Early on it opens into the extraembryonic cavity (book figure: 21 d). `[E07.02 · R06 CS9–10]` **✔✔** (R06 timing ~1–2 d later; §1)
- ✚ The first part to appear is the pericardial (heart) region, a horseshoe of cavity across the head half of the disc. `[R06 CS9]` **✚** (More; the book shows the pericardial cavity from 22 d)

### E07.02.02 Somatopleure vs splanchnopleure · AN79.4
- Parietal (somatic) layer: next to the surface ectoderm, continuous with the extraembryonic mesoderm over the amnion; with the ectoderm it is the **somatopleure** (→ body wall). `[E07.02 · R06 CS9]` **✔✔** ("somatopleuric")
- Visceral (splanchnic) layer: next to the endoderm, continuous with the extraembryonic mesoderm over the yolk sac; with the endoderm it is the **splanchnopleure** (→ gut wall). `[E07.02 · R06 CS9]` **✔✔** ("splanchnopleuric")
- The visceral layer rolls ventrally with the gut tube; the parietal layer with the ectoderm forms the lateral body-wall folds. `[E07.01, E07.02]` **✔**

### E07.03.01 Serous membranes and mesenteries · AN79.4, AN25.2
- Some parietal-layer cells become mesothelium: the parietal layer of the peritoneum, pleura and pericardium lining the cavity walls. `[E07.03 · R06 CS16]` **✔✔** (R06: coelomic surface cells become the permanent serous membranes)
- Some visceral-layer cells become the visceral layer covering the abdominal organs, lungs and heart. `[E07.03 · R06 CS14]` **✔✔** (R06: lung mesenchyme and its outer mesothelium come from the coelomic wall)
- The two layers are continuous at each organ's root — the "fist in a balloon" picture. `[E07.06]` **✔**
- The **dorsal mesentery** is a double layer of peritoneum hanging the gut from the back wall, from the caudal foregut to the end of the hindgut; mesenteries carry vessels, nerves and lymphatics to the organs. `[E07.03 · R05ao]` **✔✔** (continuous double layer; widest at stomach + duodenum) · extent **✔** book only (R05ao counts it from pharynx to anus — a wider definition, not used)
- The **ventral mesentery** exists only from the caudal foregut (lower oesophagus, stomach) to the upper duodenum; it comes from thinning of the septum transversum mesoderm. `[E07.03, E07.06]` **✔**

### E07.05.01 Diaphragm · AN52.5
- The septum transversum leaves two pericardioperitoneal canals beside the foregut, so the chest and belly cavities still connect. `[E07.04, E07.05]` **✔✔** (R06 CS13 canals)
- From the start of week 5, crescent pleuroperitoneal folds grow from the canal walls; by week 7 they are thin pleuroperitoneal membranes that fuse with the oesophageal mesentery, then with each other, covering the septum transversum. `[E07.05 · R05lg]` **✔✔** (the week-5 fold itself: R05lg, human embryos at 5 weeks, diaphragm developing in the pleuroperitoneal fold) · **✔** (the week-7 fusion sequence: book only; timing ≈ consistent with R05ai — the diaphragm cuts off the infracardiac bursa by CS17–18, 42–48 d)
- The fused membranes make the **central tendon**; their rim is connective tissue that guides incoming muscle cells. `[E07.05 · R05aa, R05lh]` **✔** (book) · **✚ (mouse)** R05aa lineage tracing and R05lh: in the mouse the folds give the central tendon and the muscle connective tissue — species-labelled, so it supports the book's answer but cannot make it ✔✔ for a human claim (B7). **Pre-existing fix for the commit runner: R05aa's own registry row says "✔✔ Ch 7, label species" and needs the same correction.** · **✚ R05lh** (**mouse**, beyond Langman — More / Tier D only): the earliest events are muscle progenitors leaving the cervical somites and phrenic-nerve axons growing in, and **both target the pleuroperitoneal folds**; the folds' **expansion** over the liver then runs ahead of muscle morphogenesis, the vascular network and phrenic-nerve branching; CDH begins before E12.5, from failure of early fold formation **or of their ability to recruit muscle**
- Muscle comes from somites at **C3–C5**; the **oesophageal mesentery** makes the **crura**. `[E07.05, E07.06]` **✔**
- The phrenic nerve (motor + sensory) comes from **C3–C5**, because the diaphragm starts opposite these neck segments in week 4. `[E07.05]` **✔**
- From week 6 the back grows faster than the front, so the diaphragm moves ventrally and caudally to its adult level and the phrenic nerves become long. `[E07.05]` **✔**
- ✚ The last part of the canal to close lies behind the adrenal, by sideways spreading of fold cells rather than simple fusion of two folds. `[R05aj]` **✚** (More / Tier D, "beyond Langman"; becomes a ⚑ candidate if a card says the folds simply fuse)
- ✚ In mice the fold cells migrate over the liver surface ahead of the muscle; muscle cells follow them. `[R05aa]` **✚** (mouse)

- **Ectopia cordis:** lateral folds fail in the chest; heart outside a cleft chest wall `[E07.03 CC]` **✔**. **Cantrell pentalogy** components — heart, pericardium, anterior diaphragm, lower sternum, supraumbilical abdominal wall `[E07.03 CC · R05ah]` **✔✔** (R05ah's heart item is a heart defect, classically intracardiac; the book names ectopia cordis); an omphalocele here is secondary to the closure defect (small belly cavity) `[E07.03 CC]` **✔**. ✚ The two sternal bars fuse in the midline only after CS20 (R05z) — More, "beyond Langman".

- **Congenital diaphragmatic hernia:** muscle fails to populate part of a pleuroperitoneal membrane because its fibroblasts give poor scaffolding or guidance cues `[E07.05 CC · R05aa]` **✔✔** (mouse: Gata4 loss in fold-derived fibroblasts → muscle-free weak patches → hernias; label species) · severe defects 85–90 % left `[E07.05 CC · R05ad]` **✔✔** (CDH Study Group, 3,754 infants: 84 % left) · organs in the chest stunt lung growth (lung hypoplasia), often fatal `[E07.05 CC · R05ac, R05aa]` **✔✔** (mortality ~38 % across 19 countries) · frequency: book **1 / 2,000** vs **2.3–2.6 / 10,000 (~1 in 4,000)** `[R05ab, R05ac]` **⚑ D-043 b**.

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decision D-043 (owner picks)
- **(a) How the ventral body wall "closes"** — book: lateral body-wall folds move ventrally and fuse in the midline (by end of week 4); defects = folds fail to progress or fuse. R05z (human embryos 4–10 wk, 3D): the muscle–rib–sternum wall spreads ventrally from CS15 to CS23; the near-constant gap between the rectus muscles shows the "closure" is mostly **differential growth of the back**, not migration and fusion; the authors challenge the migration-failure explanation of body-wall defects. (They studied the later musculoskeletal wall, not the week-4 somatopleure.)
- **(b) CDH frequency** — book 1 / 2,000; registries 2.3 / 10,000 (EUROCAT, 12 million births, R05ab) and 2.6 / 10,000 (19 countries, R05ac) ≈ 1 in 3,800–4,300. Registries count total births (live births + stillbirths + terminations), so part of the gap is definition.
- **(c) Teaching note, not a ⚑ (Tier 2 can't create one, B7):** many textbooks and Indian exam answers list the **septum transversum → central tendon** as a 4th source; Langman 14e has the pleuroperitoneal membranes make the tendon, and mouse lineage data agree (R05aa). Our K1 rows said otherwise → definitions fixed today (concepts.txt); the concept name "Diaphragm from four parts" is kept until the owner picks.
- **(d) Gastroschisis — other defects:** book: other defects in 15 %, heart defects in 5 %; R05af (3,322 cases, 24 registries): 14.1 % non-isolated, heart 2.5 %, best estimate ~10 % major unrelated defects.
Until decided: cards, Checks and scenes give the **book answer**; More carries "⚑ Newer evidence (D-043)" lines; on-screen days stay book days.

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"The gut tube and neural tube form one after the other."** They form at almost the same time in weeks 3–4 `[E07.01]` ✔.
2. **"The coelom forms between ectoderm and endoderm."** It forms **inside** the lateral plate mesoderm, splitting it in two `[E07.02 · R06 CS9]` ✔✔.
3. **"Somatopleure = somites."** Somatopleure = parietal mesoderm + ectoderm (body wall); somites are paraxial mesoderm `[E07.02, E06.02]` ✔.
4. **"Parietal and visceral peritoneum are two separate sacs."** One continuous layer, folded at each organ's root (balloon + fist) `[E07.06]` ✔.
5. **"The septum transversum alone forms the central tendon."** Langman 14e: the fused pleuroperitoneal membranes make it; the septum is the base they grow over `[E07.05 · R05aa]` ✔✔ (mouse, R05aa full text; many other textbooks list the septum as a 4th source — note D-043 c).
6. **"The septum transversum separates chest from belly."** It leaves the pericardioperitoneal canals open; the folds/membranes close them `[E07.04, E07.05 · R06 CS13]` ✔✔.
7. **"The diaphragm forms where it ends up."** It starts opposite C3–C5 and moves down; that is why the phrenic nerve is cervical and long `[E07.05]` ✔.
8. **"Diaphragm muscle comes from the septum transversum."** It comes from C3–C5 somites `[E07.05]` ✔.
9. **"Omphalocele and gastroschisis are the same thing."** Omphalocele: in the cord, amnion sac, failed midgut return, often other anomalies; gastroschisis: beside the cord (usually right), no sac, failed wall closure, rarely chromosomal `[E07.03 CC · R05ae–ag]` ✔✔.
10. **"Herniation of gut into the cord is always abnormal."** It is normal in weeks 6–10; omphalocele is when it does not return `[E07.03 CC, E08, E15]` ✔.
11. **"CDH is mostly right-sided."** About 85 % are left-sided `[E07.05 CC · R05ad]` ✔✔ (R05ad: 84 % of 3,754).
12. **"In CDH the problem is only the hole."** The killer is small lungs (hypoplasia) from organs in the chest `[E07.05 CC · R05ac]` ✔✔.
13. **"Ventral mesentery runs the whole gut."** Only lower oesophagus–stomach–upper duodenum; the dorsal mesentery runs the full length `[E07.03]` ✔.
14. **"The pleural cavities form by the lungs splitting the coelom."** The lungs grow into the canals and the body wall; the pleuropericardial membranes do the separating `[E07.04]` ✔.

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
