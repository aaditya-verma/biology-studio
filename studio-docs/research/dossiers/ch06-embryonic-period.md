## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E06.00.01 Embryonic period: weeks 3–8 · AN79.5, AN79.6
- From week 3 to week 8 each germ layer builds its own tissues and organs; by the end of month 2 the main organ systems exist and the outer body form is recognisable. `[E06.00, E06.06 · R02 CS23, R08]` **✔✔** (R02: >90 % of named adult structures present at CS23)
- This is when most major structural birth defects arise; before it, a serious insult usually ends the pregnancy. `[E06.00, E06.06 Birth Defects box]` **✔**
- Weeks 3–4 are especially vulnerable, often before a woman knows she is pregnant. `[E06.06 Birth Defects box]` **✔** (cross-ref Ch 5 misconception 7, dating)

### E06.01.01 Neural induction and the neural plate · AN79.3
- The notochord and prechordal mesoderm signal the ectoderm above them to thicken into the neural plate; its cells are neuroectoderm. `[E06.01]` **✔**
- Ectoderm left alone becomes neural; BMP4 turns it into skin. Node, notochord and prechordal mesoderm release noggin, chordin and follistatin, which block BMP4, so the midline goes neural. `[E06.01 MR, E06.06]` **✔** (Tier D wording)
- These blockers make only forebrain and midbrain tissue; hindbrain and spinal cord need WNT3a and FGF; retinoic acid helps set head-to-tail identity. `[E06.01 MR]` **✔** (Tier D)

### E06.01.02 Neurulation · AN79.3
- Neurulation turns the flat neural plate into the neural tube. `[E06.01]` **✔✔** (R05l)
- The plate lengthens by convergent extension — cells crowd in toward the midline — driven by planar cell polarity signalling. `[E06.01]` **✔** (+ R05w: PCP gene VANGL1 mutations in human NTD families — see E06.01.07)
- Its edges rise as neural folds around a central neural groove. `[E06.01 · R06 CS9]` **✔✔** (book figure days 19–20; R06 CS9 neural folds, 19–21 d)
- The folds first meet at the neck (about the 5th somite) and the seam zips both toward the head and toward the tail. `[E06.01 · R05l, R08 CS10]` **✔✔** (R05l: first fusion site "α" in the hindbrain region, near the hindbrain–neck junction, spreading both ways; R08 CS10 "neural folds begin to fuse")
- ✚ Human embryos have a **second** fusion site at the front of the forebrain ("β") that zips backward only; R05l found no convincing evidence of the mouse's multiple sites in humans (variable extra points at CS10) — others dispute this. `[R05l]` **✚** (More / Tier D, "beyond Langman"; scene follows the book: one start point)
- While closing, the tube opens to the amniotic cavity at both ends through the cranial and caudal neuropores. `[E06.01 · R05l]` **✔✔**

### E06.01.03 Neuropores close · AN79.3
- The cranial neuropore closes at about day 25 (18–20 somites). `[E06.01, E06.06 Tbl 6.4 · R08 CS11, R05l]` **✔✔** (R08: CS11, 13–20 somites, 23–26 d)
- The caudal neuropore closes at about day 28 (about 25 somites); neurulation is then complete. `[E06.01, E06.06 Tbl 6.4 · R08 CS12, R05l]` **✔✔** (R08: CS12, 21–29 somites, 26–30 d)
- The closed tube is narrow caudally (spinal cord) and broad cranially, with swellings (brain vesicles). `[E06.01]` **✔**
- ✚ The caudal neuropore's final closure lies at the level of future sacral vertebra 2; the cord below forms by a different (secondary) process. `[R05m]` **✚** (More; links to E18 spinal-cord level ⚑ candidate)

### E06.01.04 Neural crest cells · AN79.3
- Cells at the crest (edge) of the neural folds let go of their neighbours (epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition) and migrate into the mesoderm below. `[E06.01]` **✔**
- In the trunk they leave **after** the tube closes; in the head they leave **before** closure. `[E06.01]` **✔** (R05m: spinal crest from CS11)
- Trunk crest takes two routes: dorsal, under the skin → melanocytes; ventral, through the front half of each somite → sensory ganglia, sympathetic and enteric neurons, Schwann cells, adrenal medulla. `[E06.01]` **✔**
- They help build so many tissues that they are called the "fourth germ layer"; they take part in at least a third of birth defects and in cancers such as melanoma and neuroblastoma. `[E06.01]` **✔**
- (Tier D) Medium BMP at the plate–skin border, with FGF and WNT, switches on PAX3; then SNAIL and FOXD3 specify crest and SLUG drives migration. High BMP → epidermis, medium → crest, very low → neural plate. `[E06.01 MR]` **✔**

### E06.02.01 Three strips of mesoderm · AN79.4
- By about day 17 mesoderm beside the midline thickens into paraxial mesoderm; further out it stays thin as the lateral plate; intermediate mesoderm links the two. `[E06.02]` **✔** (book cross-sections days 17–21)
- Cavities in the lateral plate merge and split it into two layers. `[E06.02]` **✔**

### E06.02.02 Somites · AN79.4
- Paraxial mesoderm first forms whorl-like somitomeres, head end first; in the head they stay unsegmented and give head mesenchyme; from the occipital region down they become somites. `[E06.02]` **✔**
- The first somite pair appears in the occipital region at about day 20. `[E06.02 · R08 CS9, R05m]` **✔✔** (R08 CS9: "somites first appear", 19–21 d)
- New pairs follow head-to-tail at about three pairs a day. `[E06.02 Tbl 6.2]` **✔** (fits R06 stage somite ranges; ✚ R05q: in human stem-cell models the segmentation clock ticks about every 5 hours — in vitro, not a somite rate in the embryo)
- Because they appear so regularly, the somite count dates the embryo in weeks 3–4. `[E06.02, E06.05 · R06, R05m]` **✔✔** (R05m: somite numbers define CS9–CS12)
- Regions: **4 occipital** and **8 cervical** pairs. `[E06.02 · R05m, R05n]` **✔✔**
- **Total:** the book gives 42–44 pairs by the end of week 5 (4 occ, 8 cerv, 12 thor, 5 lumb, 5 sacr, 8–10 cocc), with the first occipital and the last 5–7 coccygeal pairs later lost. Human serial-section counts find a maximum of **38–39** pairs (CS14–16), reaching the tapered caudal end only partly. `[E06.02 · R05m, R05n]` **⚑ → D-039 (a)**

### E06.02.04 Somite differentiation · AN79.4
- A new somite is a ball of mesoderm that becomes an epithelial ring around a small cavity. `[E06.02]` **✔**
- Early in week 4 its ventral and medial wall loosens again and moves around the neural tube and notochord: this is the **sclerotome** → vertebrae and ribs. `[E06.02 · R05m]` **✔✔** (R05m: somite 1 starts to split into sclerotome and dermomyotome at CS10, 22–23 d)
- Dorsomedial and ventrolateral edge cells are muscle precursors; the cells between are the **dermatome**; together they form the **dermomyotome** → back dermis, back and body-wall muscles; ventrolateral cells also migrate into the body wall and limbs (obliques, transversus abdominis, most limb muscles). `[E06.02]` **✔**
- Each somite keeps its own segmental nerve wherever its cells go. `[E06.02]` **✔**
- (Tier D) SHH + noggin from notochord and floor plate → sclerotome (PAX1); WNT from dorsal tube → PAX3 dermomyotome and MYF5 (primaxial muscle); BMP4 + FGF from lateral plate with epidermal WNT → MYOD (ventrolateral muscle precursors); NT-3 from dorsal tube → dermis. `[E06.02 MR]` **✔**

### E06.02.05 Intermediate mesoderm · AN — none found ≈
- It forms the urogenital system: segmental nephrotomes in neck and upper chest, an unsegmented nephrogenic cord lower down → kidney excretory units and gonads. `[E06.02 · E16]` **✔**

### E06.02.06 Lateral plate mesoderm · AN79.4
- It splits into a **parietal (somatic)** layer, continuous with mesoderm on the amnion, and a **visceral (splanchnic)** layer, continuous with mesoderm on the yolk sac; between them is the intraembryonic cavity, open at first to the extraembryonic cavity. `[E06.02]` **✔**
- Parietal layer + ectoderm make the lateral body-wall folds; it forms limb and body-wall dermis, limb bones and connective tissue, and the sternum. `[E06.02]` **✔**
- Visceral layer + endoderm make the gut wall; both layers give serous membranes (peritoneum, pleura, pericardium). `[E06.02 · E07]` **✔**

### E06.03.01 Body folding · AN — none found ≈
- The growing brain and lengthening neural tube make the flat disc bulge into the amniotic cavity; head and tail folds swing ventrally and the embryo curls into the fetal position. `[E06.03]` **✔** (book: folding under way 24–25 d, tight C-shape 31–35 d)
- At the same time two lateral body-wall folds move ventrally and close the belly wall, except where the connecting stalk and yolk-sac duct stay attached (the umbilical region). `[E06.03]` **✔**
- The folds pull the amnion around the embryo, so it ends up inside the amniotic cavity. `[E06.03]` **✔**
- Failure of the lateral folds to close gives ventral body-wall defects. `[E06.03 · E07]` **✔**

### E06.03.02 Foregut, midgut, hindgut · AN — none found ≈
- Folding takes in more and more endoderm to form the gut tube: foregut, midgut and hindgut. `[E06.03]` **✔**
- The midgut stays joined to the yolk sac by the vitelline (yolk-sac) duct — wide at first, later long and thin. `[E06.03]` **✔**
- The foregut is closed at the head end by the oropharyngeal membrane, which ruptures in week 4; the hindgut is closed by the cloacal membrane, which breaks in week 7 to open the anus. `[E06.03 · R06 CS11]` **✔✔** (oropharyngeal rupture in week 4, R06 CS11) · **✔** (cloacal, week 7)
- Part of the allantois is taken into the body as the cloaca; by week 5 yolk-sac duct, allantois and umbilical vessels are gathered at the umbilical region. `[E06.03]` **✔**

### E06.03.03 Endoderm derivatives · AN — none found ≈
- Endoderm lines the gut, respiratory tract, urinary bladder and urethra, tympanic cavity and auditory tube; it forms the working cells of thyroid, parathyroids, liver and pancreas, and the tonsil and thymus stroma. `[E06.03, E06.06]` **✔**
- The yolk sac's roles: early nutrition, the first (short-lived) blood cells, and housing primordial germ cells in its back wall before they migrate to the gonads. `[E06.03 · R05o]` **✔** (R05o: the first **stem** cells arise in the embryo's aorta region, not the yolk sac — see E06.02.08)

## 5. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"The neural tube closes all at once / from one end."** It starts at the neck and zips both ways; the two ends (neuropores) close last — cranial ~day 25, caudal ~day 28 `[E06.01]` ✔✔.
2. **"Neural crest is part of the brain/spinal cord."** Crest cells leave the folds and build mostly peripheral and non-neural tissues (ganglia, melanocytes, face bones, adrenal medulla) `[E06.01 Tbl 6.1]` ✔ (GLOSSARY §4).
3. **"Neural crest is mesoderm because it makes bone and connective tissue."** It is ectoderm (neuroectoderm edge) that makes mesenchyme; mesenchyme ≠ mesoderm `[E06.01]` ✔.
4. **"Somites are segments of the neural tube / become vertebrae one-to-one."** They are paraxial mesoderm blocks beside the tube; sclerotome gives vertebrae and ribs, dermomyotome gives muscle and dermis `[E06.02]` ✔ (vertebral resegmentation: E10).
5. **"The dermatome forms the epidermis."** It forms the dermis; epidermis is ectoderm `[E06.02]` ✔.
6. **"Vasculogenesis and angiogenesis are the same."** New vessels from blood islands vs sprouts from existing vessels `[E06.02]` ✔.
7. **"Blood stem cells come from the yolk sac."** The yolk sac makes the first short-lived blood cells; definitive stem cells arise in the AGM region `[E06.02 · R05o]` ✔✔.
8. **"The whole yolk sac becomes the gut."** Only the part taken in by folding; the rest stays outside, linked by the vitelline duct `[E06.03]` ✔.
9. **"The bladder is mesoderm like the rest of the urogenital system."** Its lining is endoderm (from the cloaca); kidneys, gonads and ducts are mesoderm `[E06.03, E06.06]` ✔.
10. **"Folic acid works if started once pregnancy is confirmed."** The tube closes by about day 28 after fertilization (≈ 6 weeks after the last period), often before the pregnancy is recognised, so folic acid must start before conception `[E06.01 CC, E06.06]` ✔✔ (R05t, R05u: periconceptional).
11. **"Ectoderm = only skin."** It also makes the whole nervous system, sense epithelia, pituitary and enamel `[E06.01]` ✔.
12. **"Somite count works for the whole embryonic period."** Only in weeks 3–4; in month 2 we use crown–rump length `[E06.05]` ✔.
13. **"Limbs come from somites."** Limb bones and connective tissue come from lateral plate mesoderm; only limb muscles come from somites `[E06.02]` ✔.
14. **"The intraembryonic cavity is the gut."** The cavity is the future body cavity (coelom); the gut wall is endoderm + visceral mesoderm `[E06.02, E06.03]` ✔.
15. **"Head mesenchyme comes from somites."** In the head, paraxial mesoderm stays as unsegmented somitomeres `[E06.02]` ✔.
16. **"Anencephaly means no brain at all."** Most of the brain fails to form `[E06.01 CC]` ✔.
