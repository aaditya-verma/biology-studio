## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E05.01.01 Gastrulation in one picture · AN79.1
- Gastrulation is the week-3 process that makes three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm). `[E05.01 · R05b]` **✔✔** (R05b: human gastrula 16–19 d)
- It starts when the primitive streak appears on the epiblast. `[E05.01 · R06 CS6]` **✔✔**
- It is the start of the period most sensitive to teratogens (weeks 3–8). `[E05.05 CC · E09]` **✔**

### E05.01.02 Primitive streak · AN79.1
- The streak first shows as a faint line in the caudal midline of the disc; by about day 15–16 it is a clear narrow groove with raised edges. `[E05.01 · R06 CS6]` **✔✔** (R06: streak at ~13 d, post-ovulation; within the ±1–2 d spread)
- Its cranial end is the primitive node, a small raised area around the primitive pit. `[E05.01 · R06 CS8]` **✔✔**
- It marks the body's long axis: the head end is opposite the streak, which lies caudally. `[E05.01, E05.03]` **✔**
- It keeps adding cells until the end of week 4, then shrinks fast and disappears. `[E05.05]` **✔** (R05a: gastrulation area still present caudally to ~CS10 — consistent, not a direct confirmation)

### E05.01.03 Cells move in · AN79.1
- Epiblast cells move toward the streak, change shape, let go of their neighbours and slip underneath — this inward move is called invagination (ingression). `[E05.01]` **✔**
- The first cells in push into the hypoblast and replace it, forming the embryo's endoderm. `[E05.01]` **✔** + **✚ (R05e):** in mouse embryos the old layer is not fully pushed aside — some of its cells mix into the new gut endoderm. More / Tier D only, labelled "beyond Langman · mouse study".
- Later cells settle between epiblast and endoderm as mesoderm, spread sideways and toward the head, and meet the extraembryonic mesoderm at the disc edge. `[E05.01]` **✔**
- Cells that stay in the epiblast become ectoderm. `[E05.01]` **✔**
- (Tier D) FGF8 from streak cells loosens E-cadherin links and, with BRACHYURY (T), steers cells to mesoderm. `[E05.01]` **✔**

### E05.01.04 Three germ layers from the epiblast · AN79.1
- So all three germ layers come from the epiblast, and they build every tissue of the embryo. `[E05.01, E05.07]` **✔**
- Human data show epiblast, streak, nascent mesoderm, endoderm and primordial germ cells side by side at CS7. `[R05b, R05c]` **✚** (More / Tier D, "beyond Langman"; human lineage tracing isn't possible, so "all from epiblast" stays book-based)

### E05.02.01 Notochord · AN79.2 (+ AN79.5 nucleus pulposus, Ch 10)
- Prenotochordal cells enter at the node and move straight forward in the midline up to the prechordal plate. `[E05.02 · R05a, R06 CS7]` **✔✔**
- For a short time they sit within the endoderm layer as a notochordal plate. `[E05.02 · R05a]` **✔✔**
- The plate then lifts off the endoderm and rolls into a solid rod — the definitive notochord. `[E05.02 · R05a]` **✔✔**
- **Order and timing:** the book has the head end of the definitive notochord forming first (by ~day 17) and caudal parts added as the streak moves back. Human specimens (27 embryos, CS8–15) show the process and plate do form head-to-tail, but the definitive rod first forms **in the middle** and spreads both ways, at CS11–12 (~23–30 d). `[E05.02 · R05a]` **⚑ → D-029**
- The notochord lies under the neural tube and signals to build the axial skeleton; in adults only bits remain (nucleus pulposus). `[E05.02, E05.07 · E10]` **✔** — *misconception guard:* it does not turn into the vertebrae.

### E05.04.01 Fate map · AN79.1 (+ AN79.4)
- Where a cell passes through the node/streak decides what it becomes: cranial node → prechordal plate and notochord; node edge + cranial streak → paraxial mesoderm (somites, somitomeres); mid-streak → intermediate mesoderm (urogenital); caudal streak → lateral plate (body wall); caudal-most → extraembryonic mesoderm. `[E05.04]` **✔** (book fate map from animal mapping; R05c shows early mesoderm subtypes in human CS7 but not their entry points → no ✔✔)

### E05.05.01 Growth of the disc · AN79.1
- The flat, nearly round disc grows into a pear shape — broad head end, narrow tail end. `[E05.05 · R06 CS8, R02 CS8]` **✔✔**
- Most growth is at the head end, fed by cells streaming forward from the streak; the streak region stays about the same size. `[E05.05]` **✔**
- Head structures start to differentiate mid-week 3 while the tail end is still gastrulating (to end of week 4) — the embryo develops head-first. `[E05.05]` **✔**

## 5. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"The hypoblast becomes the endoderm."** Epiblast cells move in and replace it `[E05.01]` ✔. Nuance for More only: a few old-layer cells may mix in (mouse, ✚ R05e).
2. **"Mesoderm comes from the hypoblast / the three layers come from different starting layers."** All three come from the epiblast `[E05.01]` ✔.
3. **"Epiblast = ectoderm."** Epiblast is the week-2 layer; ectoderm is what stays behind in week 3 `[E05.01]` ✔ (GLOSSARY §4).
4. **"The primitive streak is permanent / becomes the spine."** It shrinks and vanishes by the end of week 4; leftovers can form a sacrococcygeal teratoma `[E05.05]` ✔.
5. **"The notochord becomes the vertebral column."** It signals the spine to form; only the nucleus pulposus keeps notochord cells `[E05.02 · E10]` ✔.
6. **"Streak = notochord."** The streak is where cells go in; the notochord is a rod built from some of those cells `[E05.01–02]` ✔.
7. **Dating mix-up:** week 3 after fertilization = gestational week 5 (about 4 weeks since the last period began) `[E05.05 CC]` ✔ — why gastrulation-stage exposures happen before many women know they are pregnant.
8. **"Gastrulation is over by the end of week 3."** It goes on at the tail end until the end of week 4 `[E05.05]` ✔.
9. **"Mesoderm separates ectoderm and endoderm everywhere."** Not at the oropharyngeal and cloacal membranes `[E05.01–02]` ✔.
10. **"Notochord = neural tube / spinal cord."** It lies beneath the neural plate and signals to it `[E05.02 · E06]` ✔.
