## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E02.01.01 Primordial germ cells (A, core) · AN77.3
- Eggs and sperm both come from **primordial germ cells (PGCs)**, set aside in the **epiblast** in week 2. `[E02.01]` **✔**
- During gastrulation they pass through the primitive streak and settle in the **wall of the yolk sac**, near where the umbilical cord will attach. `[E02.01 · R05hp]` **✔✔** (*human* embryos: yolk-sac wall at 3–4 weeks)
- In week 4 they start migrating back into the embryo toward the developing gonads and arrive by the **end of week 5**, dividing by mitosis on the way and after arrival. `[E02.01 · R05hp]` **✔✔** (*human*: hindgut epithelium week 4, gonad area early week 5; from week 6 along nerve fibres in the dorsal mesentery) — refutes the map misconception "germ cells start in the gonad".
- Gametogenesis = **meiosis** (halves the chromosome number) + **cytodifferentiation** (finishes the cell's shape and parts). `[E02.01]` **✔**

### E02.02.01 Mitosis (A, core) · AN73.1
- Mitosis = one cell → **two daughter cells genetically identical** to it, each with all 46 chromosomes. `[E02.02]` **✔**
- Before mitosis each chromosome **copies its DNA**; at this stage chromosomes are long and spread out, invisible under the light microscope. `[E02.02]` **✔**
- **Prophase**: chromosomes coil and condense; each now has two **chromatids** joined at the **centromere**. **Prometaphase**: chromatids become distinguishable. **Metaphase**: chromosomes line up at the equator, held by spindle microtubules. **Anaphase**: centromeres split, chromatids go to opposite poles. **Telophase**: chromosomes uncoil, the nuclear envelope reforms, the cytoplasm divides. `[E02.02]` **✔** (wording guard — note j: microtubules attach at the **kinetochore** on the centromere and run to the spindle **poles**; cards don't say "centromere to centriole")
- Homologous chromosomes never pair up in mitosis. `[E02.02]` **✔** (Fig 2.3 legend)
- *Context line, not a row fact:* the book puts the human genome at **~23,000 genes** on 46 chromosomes `[E02.02]` → **linked ⚑ D-092 (a)** (Tier 1: fewer than 20,000 protein-coding genes, R05nz). Cards follow the D-092 pick.

### E02.02.02 Meiosis (A, core) · AN77.3, AN75.4
- Meiosis is the germ-cell division: **two divisions (meiosis I and II)** take the cell from 46 to **23** chromosomes. `[E02.02]` **✔**
- The DNA is copied **once, before meiosis I**, so each of the 46 chromosomes has two sister chromatids; there is **no copying before meiosis II**. `[E02.02, E02.04]` **✔** (note k: the book says the cells replicate "at the beginning of meiosis I" — the copy is in the S phase just before)
- Meiosis I: homologous chromosomes **pair point-for-point (synapsis)** — except X with Y — then **separate**, making the cells haploid. Meiosis II: **sister chromatids separate**. `[E02.02]` **✔**
- **Crossing over happens in meiosis I**: paired homologues swap chromatid segments; the swap points show as X-shaped **chiasmata**. `[E02.02 · R05ob]` **✔✔** (*human* fetal oocytes: crossover foci counted at pachytene of prophase I) — refutes the map misconception "crossing over happens in meiosis II".
- Book: **~30–40 crossovers per meiosis I**, one or two per chromosome. `[E02.02 · R05ob]` **⚑ → D-093 (a)** — Tier 1 (*human* oocytes): mean **~70** crossovers per oocyte (range 48–102); chromosome 21 averages ~1.2 but chromosome 13 ~2.5 and the X ~3.2; men have fewer than women (same source)
- Crossovers are commonest between genes far apart on a chromosome. `[E02.02]` **✔**
- Meiosis makes every gamete different: crossing over reshuffles genes, and each pair of homologues is dealt to the daughter cells at random. `[E02.02]` **✔**
- One primary **spermatocyte** → **four** sperm (two 22+X, two 22+Y). One primary **oocyte** → **one** egg + small **polar bodies** that get almost no cytoplasm and die. `[E02.02]` **✔** (note l: the book says four cells from each oocyte, but p 29 says the first polar body only *may* divide — so two or three cells; cards say "one egg + polar bodies")

### E02.03.01 Oogenesis (A) · AN77.3
- In a genetic female, PGCs become **oogonia**, which multiply by mitosis; by the **end of month 3** they sit in clusters wrapped by flat **follicular cells** from the ovary's surface epithelium. `[E02.03]` **✔** (Ch 16 tells the same story, `[E16.02]`)
- Some oogonia stop dividing and enter **prophase of meiosis I** → **primary oocytes**. `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Germ cells peak at **~7 million in month 5**, then most die (atresia); by **month 7** nearly all oogonia are gone and the survivors are primary oocytes, each in its own layer of flat cells = a **primordial follicle**. `[E02.03]` **✔** (≈ on the number for cards — note e)
- **All eggs a woman will ever have are made before birth**; the pool only shrinks. `[E02.03 · R05oq]` **✔✔** (*human* model, 325 ovaries: the non-growing follicle pool is fixed before birth; by 30 years ~12 % of the peak is left, by 40 ~3 %) — refutes the map misconception "women make new eggs every month".
- Near birth the primary oocytes stop in the **diplotene** stage of prophase I (a resting stage, chromatin like lace) and stay there until puberty — some for **40 years or more**. `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Book: a small peptide from the follicular cells, which it calls **oocyte maturation inhibitor (OMI)**, holds the arrest. `[E02.03]` **✔** (⚑ f withdrawn at G-Sci — the Tier 1 read is *mouse* and covers only Graafian follicles; NPPC is itself a small granulosa peptide, so only the name "OMI" is dated — note w)
- ✚ *mouse* antral follicles: mural-granulosa **NPPC** → cumulus **NPR2** → **cGMP** keeps meiosis paused; without either, meiosis restarts too early. `[R05or]` **✚** More only, beyond Langman
- Book counts: **600,000–800,000** primary oocytes at birth, **~40,000** by puberty, **fewer than 500** ever ovulated. `[E02.03]` **✔** (≈ on the puberty number for cards — note e)
- Oocytes that wait longest are more likely to give a chromosomally abnormal child: the risk of chromosome errors rises with the mother's age. `[E02.03 · R05oe, R05of]` **✔✔** (Uppsala miscarriages: autosomal trisomies tied to older mothers; EUROCAT: the rise in trisomies is mainly explained by rising maternal age)
- At each cycle a **surge of LH** lets the oocyte finish **meiosis I** → a big **secondary oocyte** + a tiny **first polar body** (in the perivitelline space under the zona). The secondary oocyte enters meiosis II and **stops in metaphase II ~3 hours before ovulation**; it finishes meiosis II **only if fertilized**, otherwise it dies ~24 hours after ovulation. `[E02.03]` **✔**

### E02.03.03 Spermatogenesis (A) · AN77.3
- At birth, male germ cells sit in the **sex cords** of the testis among supporting cells that become **Sertoli cells** (from the surface epithelium, like follicular cells). `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Book: the germ cells stay as they are until just before puberty, when they give rise to **spermatogonial stem cells**; the Summary says they lie "dormant until puberty". `[E02.03, E02.04 · R05os (R05ot context)]` **⚑ → D-093 (g)** — Tier 1 (*human* biopsies): neonatal gonocytes turn into Ad (A-dark) spermatogonia by **~6 months after birth**, during "minipuberty"; the step to primary spermatocytes begins around 3 years
- Shortly before puberty the cords hollow into **seminiferous tubules**. `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Sperm making starts at puberty: stem cells → **type A spermatogonia** (a few mitoses, making clones) → **type B spermatogonia** → **primary spermatocytes** → a long prophase (~22 days) → quick meiosis I → **secondary spermatocytes** → meiosis II → **spermatids**. `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Division is incomplete, so a clone stays joined by **cytoplasmic bridges** until the sperm separate. `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Sertoli cells hold the germ cells in deep pockets, protect and feed them, and help release sperm. `[E02.03]` **✔**
- Control: **LH → Leydig cells → testosterone → Sertoli cells** → spermatogenesis; **FSH → Sertoli cells** → tubule fluid and androgen-binding proteins. `[E02.03]` **✔** (note m: the book says FSH makes "intracellular androgen receptor proteins" — standard texts say androgen-binding protein; cards say "FSH helps Sertoli cells support sperm making")
- Book: spermatogonium → mature sperm takes **~74 days**; **~300 million** sperm a day. `[E02.03, E02.04 · R05ou]` **⚑ → D-093 (h)** on the duration — a soft disagreement: the direct *human* isotope-labelling study (11 men) found labelled sperm after **64 ± 8 days (range 42–76)**, "on the shorter side" of the traditional estimates; the range includes 74. The daily count: ✔ book only (≈ for cards, note n)

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Germ cells start in the gonad."** They start in the epiblast and walk in from the yolk-sac wall `[E02.01 · R05hp]` ✔✔.
2. **"Crossing over happens in meiosis II."** It happens in prophase of meiosis I, between paired homologues `[E02.02 · R05ob]` ✔✔.
3. **"Women make new eggs every month."** The pool is made before birth and only shrinks; each month a few follicles *mature* `[E02.03 · R05oq]` ✔✔.
4. **"Spermiogenesis is another word for spermatogenesis."** It is the last step — spermatid into sperm `[E02.03]` ✔.
5. **"DNA is copied again before meiosis II."** It is copied once, before meiosis I `[E02.04]` ✔.
6. **"Meiosis I separates sister chromatids."** Meiosis I separates homologues; meiosis II separates sisters `[E02.02]` ✔.
7. **"An egg finishes meiosis at ovulation."** It ovulates stuck in metaphase II and finishes only if fertilized `[E02.03]` ✔.
8. **"One oocyte makes four eggs, like sperm."** One egg + polar bodies; one spermatocyte → four sperm `[E02.02]` ✔.
9. **"Down syndrome is always free trisomy 21."** ~4 % translocation, ~1 % mosaic `[E02.02 CC]` ✔.
10. **"The extra 21 comes from either parent equally."** Overwhelmingly the mother's (⚑ b on how much) `[E02.02 CC · R05oc]`.
11. **"Prader–Willi and Angelman are different deletions."** Same region, 15q11–q13 — the parent decides (imprinting) `[E02.02 CC · R05om, -13]` ✔✔.
12. **"A balanced translocation carrier is ill."** They are usually healthy but can have unbalanced children `[E02.02 CC]` ✔.
13. **"Turner girls are mostly miscarried, so all 45,X die."** It is the one monosomy that can survive to birth `[E02.02 CC]` ✔.
14. **"A healthy parent can't pass on a new mutation to several children."** Germline mosaicism can `[E02.02 CC]` ✔.
15. **"Exome sequencing reads the whole genome."** Only the ~1 % that codes for protein `[E02.02 CC]` ✔.
16. **"Male germ cells do nothing from birth to puberty."** They become spermatogonia in the first months after birth (⚑ g) `[R05os]`.
