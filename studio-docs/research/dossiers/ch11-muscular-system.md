## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E11.00.01 Where muscles come from · AN3.1, AN79.4
- Almost all muscle is **mesoderm**. Three kinds: skeletal, cardiac and smooth. `[E11.00, E11.10]` **✔**
- **Skeletal muscle comes from paraxial mesoderm:** somites for the neck, trunk, body wall and limbs; somitomeres (with the occipital somites) for the head. `[E11.00, E11.01, E11.05]` **✔✔** (R05dg review, *chick + mouse* lineage maps: all postcranial striated muscle, in axial and limb regions alike, comes from somites; somitic cells invade the lateral plate to make limb and body-wall muscle)
- **Limb muscles are somite cells that migrated into the limb bud.** The bones and connective tissue of the limb come from lateral plate mesoderm, not from the somites. `[E11.07 · R05dg, R05dp]` **✔✔** (R05dg, *chick*: somites from any axial level, put at limb level, fill the bud with a normal set of muscles; R05dp, *human*: myoblasts already in the arm bud at CS14)
- Inside the somite: the ventromedial wall becomes **sclerotome** (vertebrae, ribs; Ch 10). The dorsal part becomes the **dermomyotome**; cells from its **dorsomedial (DML) and ventrolateral (VLL) lips** make the muscle precursors that form the **myotome** under it, and the rest becomes **dermatome** (dermis of the back). `[E11.01, E11.10 · R05m]` **✔✔** (R05m, *human* serial sections: sclerotome from CS10) · lips ✔ (no human source retrieved; §10)
- **Cardiac muscle** comes from splanchnic (visceral) mesoderm around the heart tube. **Gut smooth muscle** comes from splanchnic mesoderm around the gut. `[E11.00, E11.08, E11.09]` **✔**
- **The exceptions:** smooth muscle of the **pupil** (sphincter and dilator) and the contractile cells of the **mammary and sweat glands** come from **ectoderm**; smooth muscle of the dorsal aorta and large arteries from lateral plate mesoderm and **neural crest**; of the coronary arteries from **proepicardial** cells and crest. `[E11.00, E11.09]` **✔**

### E11.02.03 Dermatomes and myotomes keep their nerve · AN4.1, AN7.5, AN79.4, AN24.4
- **Each myotome is supplied by the spinal nerve of its own segment**, whichever domain its cells end up in, primaxial or abaxial. `[E11.01, E11.02]` **✔** (no Tier 1 source on segment-matched supply retrieved; R05dn, *human*, shows only that the fused epaxial column at CS16 gets dorsal branches)
- **The nerve goes where the muscle goes.** A muscle that migrates far keeps its home segment's nerve, so the nerve is dragged along. The book's own example: the **phrenic nerve (C3–C5)** supplies the diaphragm low in the trunk because its muscle starts in the neck (Ch 7). `[E11.11, E07.05]` **✔** (Ch 7 dossier, E07.05.01)
- **Because of resegmentation, each myotome bridges an intervertebral disc** and attaches to two vertebrae, which is what lets segmental muscles move the spine — the other half of Ch 10's story. **Not re-argued here:** see E10.02.01 (Ch 10 dossier §2 and §5 c — human R05cj, chick R05ck/R05cl, axolotl R05dd). `[E10.02 · R05cl]` **✔✔**
- The lateral somitic frontier is also the border between back dermis (from dermatomes) and body-wall dermis (from lateral plate). `[E11.01]` **✔**
- Each segment's skin area (the adult dermatome) is supplied by that segment's sensory nerve. Ch 11 does not say this; it belongs with the nerve chapters. `[— · R05ma]` **✚** beyond Langman (R05ma, *human* systematic review: an evidence-based map of the skin each dorsal root reliably supplies; neighbouring dermatomes **overlap** and vary between people, and the usual textbook maps rest on weak studies — a card says "about", never a hard border)
- **Dorsal ramus → epaxial (true back) muscles; ventral ramus → hypaxial muscles (body wall and limbs).** This is a rule about *innervation*, not about origin. `[E11.02, E11.10 · R05dn]` **✔✔** dorsal half (R05dn, *human*) · ventral half **✔**

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decision D-066 (owner picks)
- **(a) When muscle fibres become striated.** Book: cross-striations by the end of the third month. *Human* electron microscopy: arm muscle shows a striated pattern at **CS22** (days ~54–56, R05dp); extraocular muscle has A-bands with Z- and M-lines at **CS21** (R05dq). That is the end of the embryonic period, about a month earlier than the book. Mature features (T-tubules, triads) follow at fetal weeks 11–12 (R05dp), which may be what "typical striations by month 3" refers to. Options: (1) default — book month; (2) card says "striations appear by the end of week 8 and the fibres mature through month 3", book figure in More. *Recommended (2).*
- **(b) What BMP4 does to the VLL lip.** Book text: BMP4 (and probably FGFs) from lateral plate, with ectodermal WNTs, signal VLL cells to express MyoD. *Chick* (R05dh, R05di): lateral-plate BMP4 **lateralises** the somite (gives it lateral identity) but **inhibits and delays** MyoD and MYF5; noggin at the DML lip releases the block, and ectopic noggin makes a lateral myotome. The book's own somite figure legend calls BMP4 inhibitory, so the book is internally mixed. Tier D only. Options: (1) default; (2) card says "BMP4 from the lateral plate marks VLL cells as lateral and holds their muscle genes back; WNTs switch MyoD on", book line in More. *Recommended (2)* — it is also what the book's figure shows.
- **(c) Which muscles and rib parts are primaxial or abaxial — species-dependent, unknown in humans.** This is the book's (chick-derived) lists vs mouse, not book vs human evidence. The concept is solid (R05de *chick*, R05df *mouse*, R05dg review). The **lists** differ by species: the book's sternal-rib cartilage = abaxial matches *chick*; in *mouse*, most ribs are entirely primaxial (only rib 1 has an abaxial part), and the intercostals, latissimus dorsi, spinotrapezius and scapula each have primaxial and abaxial parts. There is **no human map** (R05dg: the frontier is only preliminarily mapped, in chick and mouse). The same rib line sits on Ch 10's E10.03.01 (✔ there). Options: (1) default — book lists in the card and Check; (2) card teaches the frontier and "primaxial ≠ epaxial" (intercostals as the example, as the book lists them), keeps the book's lists in More with "mapped in animals; muscles like latissimus dorsi straddle the line in mice", and no Check asks to sort a named muscle; (3) as (2) and also reword E10.03.01's rib line the same way. *Recommended (3).*
- **(d) How common Duchenne muscular dystrophy is.** Book: 1 in 4,000 male births. R05dt (systematic review, 40 studies): birth prevalence **19.8 per 100,000 live male births ≈ 1 in 5,000** (95 % CI 16.6–23.6 ≈ 1 in 4,200–6,000; very high heterogeneity, no high-quality study). The book's figure is just above the interval. Options: (1) default; (2) card says "about 1 in 4,000–5,000 male births". *Recommended (2).*
- **(e) Prune belly syndrome — what it is and why.** Book: absence of abdominal muscle, **sometimes** associated with urinary-tract and bladder malformations including urethral obstruction, whose fluid distends the abdomen and causes the muscle atrophy. Tier 1: it is **defined by a triad** — abdominal-muscle deficiency, urinary-tract abnormalities and undescended testes (R05dw, R05dx; "bilateral" R05dx; the testis part applies to boys); **~95 % are boys** (R05dw); 3.8 per 100,000 live births in a US inpatient study of male newborns, with 29 % in-hospital mortality (R05dv); the cause is **unknown** — the obstruction theory is one of several, alongside a primary mesoderm defect, and variants in genes for genitourinary muscle development have been found (R05dw, R05dx). Options: (1) default; (2) card names the triad, says "mostly boys" and "cause still debated — one idea is early blockage of urine flow", book mechanism in More. *Recommended (2)* — "sometimes associated" would mislead an exam answer.

Until decided: cards, Checks and scenes give the **book answer**; More carries "⚑ Newer evidence (D-066)" lines; every age is labelled with its clock.

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Limb muscles grow out of the limb bud's own tissue."** The muscle cells are somite cells that migrated in; only the bones, tendons and connective tissue are lateral plate `[E11.07 · R05dg, R05dp]` ✔✔.
2. **"Primaxial means epaxial."** They answer different questions. Primaxial/abaxial = which connective tissue the muscle forms in (origin); epaxial/hypaxial = which ramus supplies it. In the book's classification the **rhomboids and the intercostals are primaxial, yet both are supplied by ventral rami** `[E11.02 · R05dg]` ✔ (lists: ⚑ D-066 c — use the rhomboids in Checks).
3. **"Limb muscles are epaxial and supplied by dorsal rami."** (map row) All limb muscles, flexor and extensor, are hypaxial and supplied by **ventral** rami; the "dorsal" muscle mass of a limb is not a back muscle `[E11.02, E11.10]` ✔.
4. **"The lateral somitic frontier separates somite cells from lateral plate cells."** Somite muscle cells cross it. It separates connective tissue of somite origin from connective tissue of lateral-plate origin; abaxial muscle is somite cells in lateral-plate tissue `[E11.01 · R05de, R05dg]` ✔✔ (*chick* + *mouse*).
5. **"A muscle is supplied by the nerve of the place it ends up."** It keeps its **home segment's** nerve and drags it along: the phrenic nerve (C3–C5) to the diaphragm `[E11.02, E11.11, E07.05]` ✔.
6. **"Each segment's muscle belongs to one vertebra."** After resegmentation each myotome **bridges a disc** and pulls on two vertebrae — that is how the spine bends (Ch 10, E10.02.01) `[E10.02 · R05cl]` ✔✔ (*chick*).
7. **"The muscle cells decide a muscle's shape."** The connective tissue they move into does; somite cells are not committed to a particular muscle `[E11.04 · R05dg]` ✔✔ (*chick*).
8. **"Head muscles come from neural crest."** They are paraxial mesoderm; crest makes the connective tissue that patterns them (and the face skeleton, Ch 10) `[E11.05 · R05dm]` ✔✔.
9. **"All smooth muscle is splanchnic mesoderm."** Pupil, mammary and sweat-gland muscle is ectoderm; vessel walls are lateral plate and crest; coronary arteries proepicardium `[E11.09]` ✔.
10. **"Cardiac muscle cells fuse like skeletal ones."** They stay single cells joined by intercalated discs; Purkinje fibres are modified muscle, not nerve `[E11.08]` ✔.
11. **"One muscle fibre is one cell from one myoblast."** A skeletal fibre is many myoblasts fused, so it has many nuclei `[E11.06 · R05dp]` ✔✔.
12. **"MyoD is *the* muscle switch."** MYF5 and MyoD back each other up; only losing both removes skeletal muscle (*mouse*) `[E11.03 · R05dj, R05dk]` ✚ *(More / Tier D only)*.
13. **"A missing muscle is always serious."** About 1 in 5 people lack a palmaris longus, usually with no deficit `[E11.09 CC · R05du]` ✔✔.
14. **"Prune belly is only a muscle problem."** It is a triad with the urinary tract and undescended testes, nearly always in boys `[E11.09 CC · R05dw]` ⚑ D-066 e. *(Checks only if D-066 e (2) is chosen; until then More only.)*
15. **"Duchenne and Becker are different genes."** Same gene, dystrophin: none made in DMD, some in BMD — hence later, milder BMD `[E11.09 CC]` ✔.
16. **"Striated muscle looks striated only after the third month."** Human embryos show striation by the end of week 8 `[E11.06 · R05dp, R05dq]` ⚑ D-066 a. *(Checks only if D-066 a (2) is chosen; until then More only.)*

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
- **Two classifications with similar names.** Primaxial/abaxial (origin) and epaxial/hypaxial (nerve) overlap for most muscles and differ for a few — the intercostals are the test case. Teach them on one cross-section, with one Check that asks for both labels.
- **"Origin" means three things here.** The muscle cell (somite), the connective tissue (somite or lateral plate or crest) and the nerve (home segment). Name which origin a sentence means.
- **Moving muscles, fixed nerves.** The phrenic nerve and the limb plexuses make sense only once the "nerve follows the muscle" rule is seen in motion — animate it once.
- **Animal data in a human chapter.** Most of the mechanism is chick and mouse; the human sources give timing only. Every species is labelled.
