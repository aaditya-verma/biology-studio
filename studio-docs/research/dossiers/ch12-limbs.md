## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E12.01.01 Limb buds · AN13.8, AN20.10, AN79.4
- **Limbs appear at the end of week 4** as small swellings on the side of the body wall; the **arm bud comes first, the leg bud 1–2 days later**, and the leg stays about 1–2 days behind the arm all through. `[E12.01, E12.03 · R05ds]` **✔✔** for the fourth-week appearance (R05ds, *human*: limbs emerge in the fourth post-conception week) · **✔** for the 1–2-day arm-before-leg lag (book only)
- **What a bud is made of:** a core of mesenchyme from the **parietal (somatic) layer of lateral plate mesoderm**, which becomes the limb's **bones and connective tissue**, covered by ectoderm. The **muscle cells are not from the bud** — they migrate in from somites (Ch 11, E11.07.01). `[E12.01, E12.03 · R05dg, R05dp]` **✔✔**
- **Each limb is built in three blocks, proximal to distal:** **stylopod** (humerus / femur), **zeugopod** (radius + ulna / tibia + fibula), **autopod** (wrist and fingers / ankle and toes). `[E12.01, E12.03]` **✔**
- **Where a limb grows is set by HOX genes** along the head-to-tail axis; **TBX5** specifies forelimb and **TBX4 with PITX1** hind limb. `[E12.01, E12.02, E12.03 · R05ej, R05ek]` **✔** · **✔✔** for TBX5 ↔ upper limb (R05ej, *human*: TBX5 mutations cause Holt–Oram, upper limb + heart) · ✚ TBX4 ↔ hind limb in humans comes from clubfoot genetics (R05ek, *human* 17q23 duplications containing TBX4)
- The **AER** is a thickening of ectoderm at the bud tip; it keeps the mesenchyme under it in an **undifferentiated, fast-dividing zone**, and cells that fall behind that influence start turning into cartilage and muscle. So the limb is laid down **proximal first**. The book says those cells become cartilage **and muscle**; the muscle cells are in fact somite immigrants (Ch 11), so read it as "cartilage, and the tissue the muscle grows in". `[E12.01]` **✔** (the book's AER picture is *chick*; it states week 5 for humans in words)

### E12.01.02 Shaping and rotating the limbs · AN13.8, AN20.10
- **Week 6: the tip flattens into a hand plate and a foot plate**, marked off by a groove; a second groove later divides the proximal part, so the three blocks become visible. `[E12.01]` **✔**
- **Two things happen at once.** Cell death splits the AER into **five** segments (day 48); each ray then **keeps growing** under its own segment of ridge while its mesenchyme condenses; and the tissue **between** the rays dies (day 51), separation complete by **day 56**. The hand is carved as much as it is grown — the carving is the part learners miss. `[E12.01, E12.03]` **✔**
- **Apoptosis is normal, necessary development** — the same programmed cell death that shapes the hand also shapes the joint cavities (below). When interdigital death fails, the digits stay joined (**syndactyly**, E12.02.04). `[E12.01, E12.02 CC]` **✔**
- **In week 7 the two limbs rotate in opposite directions:** upper ~90° **laterally** → extensors lie posterolaterally, thumb lateral; lower ~90° **medially** → extensors lie anteriorly, big toe medial. This is why the knee points forward and the elbow backward, and why limb dermatomes spiral. The book's clock here is "the seventh week of gestation", undefined — we read it as after fertilization (D-030, ⚑ D-067 a). `[E12.01]` **✔** (the rotation itself is not confirmed in a human imaging source — §10)
- **Joints come from interzones.** Where two cartilage models meet, chondrogenesis stops and an **interzone** forms; its cells condense, a **cavity opens by cell death**, the **interzone's own tissue** becomes the articular cartilage, synovial membrane, menisci and the ligaments inside the capsule, and the mesenchyme **around** it becomes the capsule. **Fibrous joints** (skull sutures, Ch 10) form from interzones that never cavitate. The book names **WNT14** as the likely inducing signal and says the positioning rule is not settled. `[E12.01]` **✔**
- **Bones of the limb form by endochondral ossification** from lateral plate mesoderm: cartilage model → primary centre in the shaft → secondary centres in the epiphyses → growth plates (details E12.01.03, mechanism shared with Ch 10's E10.01.01). `[E12.01, E12.03]` **✔** (mechanism cross-checked in Ch 10's E10.01.01; no Ch 12 Tier 1 source retrieved for the human endochondral sequence — §10)

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decision D-067 (owner picks)
- **(a) When thalidomide does its damage, and on which clock.** Book: the **fourth and fifth weeks of gestation** are the most sensitive period for limb defects — without saying whether "gestation" is counted from fertilization or from the last period, a difference of two weeks. Tier 1 (R05cd, the Swedish thalidomide cohort): the sensitive phase is **20–36 days after conception**, and the kind of defect tracks where in that window the exposure fell. 20–36 days after conception is late week 3 to week 5 after fertilization — i.e. it **starts before** the limb bud is visible and matches the book's figure only if the book means weeks after fertilization. Options: (1) default — book wording; (2) card says "about **20–36 days after fertilization** (roughly weeks 3–5), the bud-stage window", with the book's phrasing in More and an explicit clock label. *Recommended (2)* — the clock is exactly what an exam answer gets wrong.
- **(b) Whether retinoic acid is the proximal signal and the ZPA's trigger.** Book: flank mesenchyme makes retinoic acid, which specifies the stylopod (MEIS1 as marker), and the ZPA's cells make retinoic acid, which initiates SHH. Tier 1 (R05eb, *mouse* Raldh2/Raldh3 nulls): embryos that cannot make RA **still express limb SHH and MEIS2**; RA is required **outside** the limb field, where it represses trunk FGF8 and so permits **forelimb induction**; hind-limb budding and patterning happen without it, and the fin defect in RA-deficient *zebrafish* is rescued by an FGF-receptor blocker. Tier D only. Options: (1) default; (2) card keeps the book's proximal-to-distal story but says "RA's established job is **permitting the forelimb bud to start**, by holding down FGF8 in the trunk; its role as a proximal patterning signal inside the limb is disputed", book line in More; (3) drop RA from the card and teach FGF/SHH/WNT7a only. *Recommended (2).*
- **(c) How a digit knows which digit it is.** Book: SHH from the ZPA is a concentration-dependent morphogen specifying the anteroposterior order. Tier 1 (R05eh, *mouse* genetic fate mapping): the **descendants of the SHH-producing cells themselves make up the two most posterior digits** and part of the middle one, and the **duration of exposure** separates the posterior digits, while concentration separates the anterior ones. Tier D only. Options: (1) default; (2) card says "the nearest digits are made of ZPA cells themselves and are told apart by **how long** they saw SHH; the far digits by **how much**", book line in More. *Recommended (2)* — it also explains the mirror-image graft result better.
- **(d) How common limb defects are.** Book: about **6 per 10,000 live births** (3.4 upper, 1.1 lower) and **syndactyly 1 in 2,000 births**. **No population-based Tier 1 figure was retrieved for either** (R05el is a registry study of *causes*, not rates; R05em is a school survey, not a birth registry). This is a **gap, not a contradiction** — but the numbers are quotable in exams, so the owner should decide how a card handles them. ## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Arms and legs rotate the same way."** (map row) They rotate **opposite** ways in week 7: the arm laterally, the leg medially. That is why the elbow points back and the knee points forward `[E12.01, E12.03]` ✔.
2. **"Fingers grow out of the hand plate like branches."** They are **carved out**: the AER breaks into five, then the tissue between the rays dies. Development here is subtraction `[E12.01]` ✔.
3. **"Apoptosis means something went wrong."** The same programmed death makes the digits **and** the joint cavities; failure of it causes syndactyly `[E12.01, E12.02 CC]` ✔.
4. **"The AER makes the limb pattern."** The AER keeps the tip growing (FGFs); the **ZPA** sets thumb-to-little-finger order (SHH), **WNT7a** sets back-versus-palm, and **HOX** genes set the bone shapes. Three jobs, three signals `[E12.02, E12.03]` ✔.
5. **"The ZPA tells digits apart purely by concentration."** In mice the posterior digits are made **of ZPA descendants** and are told apart by **time of exposure** `[E12.02 · R05eh]` ⚑ D-067 c. *(Checks only if D-067 c (2) is chosen; until then More only.)*
6. **"Limb muscle grows from the limb bud."** (Ch 11 dossier §6.1) Muscle cells migrate in from somites; only bone, tendon and connective tissue are lateral plate `[E12.02, E11.07 · R05dg, R05dp]` ✔✔.
7. **"The dorsal muscle mass of a limb is a back muscle."** (map row on E11.02.02) The dorsal mass is the **extensor** group; it and the ventral (flexor) group are both **hypaxial and abaxial**, both supplied by **ventral** rami. No limb muscle has a dorsal-ramus supply `[E12.02, E11.02]` ✔.
8. **"The radial nerve is a 'dorsal' nerve because it lies at the back."** It is dorsal because it is built from the **dorsal branches** of several ventral rami — an embryonic address, not an adult position `[E12.02]` ✔.
9. **"Limb dermatomes are a mess."** They are strictly segmental, C5–T2 and L2–S2; the **rotation** of the limb is what makes the adult strips look spiral `[E12.02, E12.03]` ✔ · ✚ real people vary in nerve branching (R05ei), so a map is an average.
10. **"One adult muscle comes from one somite."** Splitting and fusion mix segments, so most limb muscles draw on several — which is why a single nerve root lesion weakens many muscles partly, not one muscle totally `[E12.02]` ✔.
11. **"HOX genes only set where the limb is."** They set position along the body **and** the type and shape of the bones in each block (HOXA/HOXD) — a HOXD13 mutation gives syndactyly with polydactyly `[E12.02, E12.02 CC]` ✔.
12. **"Retinoic acid is vitamin A, so it is only a vitamin."** It is a signalling molecule in limb induction, and in excess it is a **teratogen**; whether it patterns the limb from inside is disputed `[E12.02 · R05eb]` ⚑ D-067 b. *(Checks only if D-067 b (2) is chosen.)*
13. **"Thalidomide caused defects whenever it was taken in pregnancy."** The damaging window was narrow — about **20–36 days after conception** `[E12.02 CC · R05cd]` ⚑ D-067 a. *(Until decided, Checks use the book's wording.)*
14. **"Thalidomide is banned."** It is in use (leprosy complications, myeloma); that is exactly why its distribution is controlled in pregnancy `[E12.02 CC]` ✔.
15. **"Amniotic bands are a malformation of the limb."** The limb formed normally and was then **disrupted** by a constricting band — different mechanism, different counselling `[E12.02 CC · R05eg]` ✔✔.
16. **"Phocomelia means no limb."** **Amelia** is no limb; **phocomelia** is a form of **meromelia** — hands or feet attached close to the trunk `[E12.02 CC]` ✔.
17. **"Congenital hip dislocation means the hip was dislocated in the womb."** The **socket** is underdeveloped before birth; the dislocation itself usually happens **after** birth `[E12.02 CC · R05ef]` ✔✔.
18. **"Bones grow from the middle outwards for life."** The shaft ossifies first, but **length** comes from the growth plates, which close in adolescence `[E12.01]` ✔.

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
- **Three axes at once.** Proximodistal (AER/FGF), anteroposterior (ZPA/SHH), dorsoventral (WNT7a/LMX1). Teach them as three questions about the same bud — *how long? which side? which face?* — on one diagram, one axis at a time.
- **Rotation breaks the mental model.** Every "why is the knee the other way round?" answer depends on week 7. Animate the two rotations once, then reuse that frame for dermatomes and for flexor/extensor compartments.
- **Subtraction, not addition.** Learners expect digits to grow out. Apoptosis as a *building* tool is the chapter's counter-intuitive idea and the one its defects hang off.
- **Segments that stop looking segmental.** C5–T2 and L2–S2 are tidy; adult muscles and dermatomes are not. Name the two things that blur them: splitting/fusion of muscle masses, and rotation.
- **Animal data in a human chapter.** Almost all the molecular work is chick and mouse (the book's own AER figure is a chick). Every species is labelled.
