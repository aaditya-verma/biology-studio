## 1. Staging & scale — the sensitive-period chart as our own staging (RULES B2)
Ch 9 gives no new embryo days or sizes; what it gives is a **risk-against-time** curve. We redraw it as our own staging strip (never the book's figure, G1). Carnegie stages for the window come from the registry (R08 classic ranges, D-030), not from the book.

| Window (after fertilization) | Carnegie | What the book says happens `[section]` | Risk of a structural defect | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 (days 1–14) | CS1–CS6 | Older teaching: "all or none" — the embryo either dies or recovers with no ill effect. | **Weeks 3–8 (days 15–56)** | **CS7–CS23** | **Organogenesis**: organ systems are laid down, each with one or more windows of its own. | Week 9 → birth (fetal period) | — (past CS23) | Organs have formed, so gross structural defects become uncommon; **differentiation continues, especially in the brain**, so exposures can still cause learning and motor disability `[E09.01]` | Falling for structure, still real for function | ✔ |
| Whole of pregnancy | — | **No stage is completely safe** `[E09.01, E09.04]` | — | ✔ |

**The prevention point the chart exists to make:** most defects are induced before week 8 after fertilization, but most women do not attend a first prenatal visit until about then — so prevention has to start **before conception** `[E09.01, E09.01 CC, E09.04]` **✔**.

**One sensitivity window is not one organ:** the book's worked example is cleft palate, inducible at the blastocyst (day 6), at gastrulation (day 14), at the early limb-bud stage (week 5) and when the palatal shelves form (week 7) `[E09.01]` **✔**.

## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E09.01.02 Principles of teratology · AN79.5, AN79.6
- **Susceptibility depends on genotype** — the conceptus's, and the mother's too (she metabolises the drug, resists or fails to resist the infection), so the same exposure does not give the same outcome in two pregnancies. `[E09.01]` **✔**
- **Susceptibility depends on when** the exposure lands: weeks 3–8 after fertilization is organogenesis and carries most structural defects, but each organ has its own window or windows, and no stage is wholly safe. `[E09.01 · R05cd]` **✔✔** (R05cd: thalidomide's sensitive phase is 20–36 days after conception, with the earliest-window cases carrying the eye findings — a window measured in a human cohort)
- **Manifestation depends on dose and duration.** `[E09.01]` **✔** · ✚ dose-dependence is now measured for antiseizure drugs: major malformations rose with the dose at conception for valproate, carbamazepine, lamotrigine and phenobarbital in a 7,355-pregnancy prospective registry `[R05bu]` **✚**
- **Teratogens act by specific mechanisms** on cells and tissues (blocking one biochemical or molecular step), and the pathway to the defect runs through cell death, reduced proliferation or similar. `[E09.01]` **✔**
- **The outcomes are four:** death, malformation, growth restriction, and functional disorder. `[E09.01]` **✔**
- Weeks 1–2 are **not** a safe "all-or-none" window: axis specification begins at the blastocyst late in week 1, and errors there can produce virtually any defect. `[E09.01]` **✔** (the book's own correction of older teaching — this is the chapter's headline misconception, §6.1)
- The **critical period for most structural defects therefore runs from fertilization through week 8**, not from week 3. `[E09.01]` **✔**

### E09.01.04 Preventing birth defects · AN79.5
- **Folic acid before conception** lowers the rate of neural tube defects, and also reduces hyperthermia-related abnormalities. `[E09.01 CC · R05bs, R05bt]` **✔✔** (R05bs, randomised, 1,195 completed pregnancies in women with a previous affected pregnancy: 72 % fewer NTDs with folic acid, RR 0.28; the other seven vitamins did nothing. R05bt: after mandatory US fortification, reported spina bifida fell 31 % and anencephaly 16 %, and NTD-affected pregnancies fell from ~4,000 to ~3,000 a year)
- **Iodised salt** removes the intellectual disability and bone deformity of cretinism. `[E09.01 CC]` **✔**
- **Tight metabolic control before conception** in diabetes and in PKU lowers the defect rate towards the general-population rate. `[E09.01, E09.01 CC]` **✔**
- **Avoiding alcohol at every stage** of pregnancy lowers the defect rate. `[E09.01 CC]` **✔**
- Every prevention strategy has one thing in common: it has to **start before conception**. `[E09.01 CC, E09.04]` **✔**
- Prescribers must treat any woman who could become pregnant as possibly pregnant — the book's worked case is the hundreds of children damaged by retinoids given for cystic acne, a drug prescribed to young, often sexually active patients. `[E09.01 CC]` **✔**

| E09.01.01 Types of birth defects (B) | **Birth defect = congenital malformation = congenital anomaly**: a structural, behavioural, functional or metabolic disorder present at birth; the study of them is **teratology** / **dysmorphology** · **major** structural anomalies in ~3 % of live-born infants; defects cause ~25 % of infant deaths, are the 5th leading cause of years of potential life lost before 65, and occur at the **same frequency across ethnic groups** · **minor** anomalies (small ears, pigmented spots, short palpebral fissures) in ~15 % of newborns: harmless themselves but a clue — one minor anomaly carries a 3 % chance of a major one, two 10 %, three or more 20 %; ear anomalies are the most useful clue and appear in nearly every syndromic child · causes split ~15 % purely environmental, ~30 % purely genetic, ~55 % multifactorial (gene–teratogen interaction, plus defects of unknown origin) · **five words that are not synonyms**: **malformation** (something formed wrongly, genetic and/or environmental), **disruption** (a formed structure destroyed — amniotic bands, vascular accidents causing transverse limb defects), **deformation** (mechanical moulding over time — clubfeet from compression, often reversible after birth, usually musculoskeletal), **syndrome** (anomalies with one known cause: a diagnosis, so recurrence risk is known), **association** (anomalies that co-occur more often than chance with no cause found — VACTERL: vertebral, anal atresia, cardiac, tracheo-oesophageal fistula, renal, limb) | `[E09.01, E09.04]` | ✔ (the 3 % / 25 % / 15 % figures are the book's own, Tier 1 check still open — §10 to-do) |

microcephaly, visual impairment, intellectual disability, fetal death; some infants normal at birth then develop hearing loss, visual impairment and intellectual disability · **Herpes simplex**: rare as a teratogen, usually transmitted during delivery · **Varicella**: skin scarring, limb hypoplasia, eye and CNS defects; | E09.01.03 Key teratogens — social drugs (B) | **Alcohol**: the book gives FAS 2–9 per 1,000 births and FASD 24–48 per 1,000, with FAS + ARND together ~1 in 100; **FAS** is the severe end (growth < 10th centile, head circumference 10th centile, two of three facial features — short palpebral fissures, smooth philtrum, thin upper lip — always some cognitive disorder); **FASD** covers any alcohol-related defect; **ARND** is CNS involvement without the FAS criteria; alcohol is the leading cause of intellectual disability; dose and timing both matter and there is probably **no safe level**, with binge drinking at a critical stage raising the risk of orofacial clefts; mechanism (recent evidence, **animal and cell data only** — R05lv): alcohol down-regulates **sonic hedgehog** by interfering with its binding to cholesterol, so brain development fails and neural crest cells die — in zebrafish, blocking the cholesterol modification lowers mature membrane-bound Shh and produces lasting FAS-like malformations, and in a rat **hepatic stellate** cell line (HSC8B — not a neural cell type) alcohol stops Shh entering Golgi-to-membrane transport vesicles and cuts the caveolin-1/Shh complex in lipid rafts, so Shh is not secreted; **no human evidence**, so the mechanism is ✚ and species-labelled wherever it is taught · **cocaine**: preterm labour, growth restriction, abortion; heart, genitourinary and brain defects; behavioural effects later; confounded because users often also drink · **marijuana (THC)**: some studies link first-4-weeks use to anencephaly, others to ADHD, learning and memory problems; also preterm birth and low birth weight · **cigarettes**: orofacial clefts, and a moderate risk factor for heart defects (TGA, ASD, pulmonary stenosis, common truncus); growth restriction, preterm delivery, abortion, stillbirth; after birth, low birth weight, SIDS, asthma, respiratory infection · **LSD and PCP**: a review of > 100 publications concluded pure LSD at moderate dose is **not** teratogenic, and PCP evidence is likewise inconclusive | `[E09.01 · R05by, R05lv]` | ✔ · **✚ R05lv** (zebrafish + a rat hepatic stellate cell line — beyond Langman, More / Tier D only) for the Shh mechanism · **⚑ D-057 a** (FASD rate: R05by, active case-finding in 6,639 first-graders across 4 US communities — conservative FASD prevalence 11.3–50.0 per 1,000, i.e. 1.1–5.0 %, and weighted estimates to 98.5 per 1,000, above the book's 24–48 per 1,000) |
| E09.01.03 Key teratogens — hormones, IVF, maternal disease (B) | **Androgenic progestins** (ethisterone, norethisterone, once given to prevent miscarriage) masculinise female genitalia — enlarged clitoris, fused labioscrotal folds · **DES** — vaginal and cervical carcinoma in exposed daughters, malformed uterus, tubes and upper vagina; in exposed sons, malformed testes and abnormal sperm, but no rise in genital carcinoma · **endocrine disrupters** (industrial chemicals, pesticides) act mostly through the oestrogen receptor; falling sperm counts and rising testicular cancer, hypospadias and reproductive-tract defects, plus CNS changes in heavily exposed animals, are why they are watched · **oral contraceptives** — low teratogenic potential, but stop them if pregnancy is suspected · **cortisone** — cleft palate reliably in mice and rabbits; human studies suggest a modest rise in orofacial clefts · **IVF** — more birth defects, higher still with ICSI; any infertility treatment, including ovulation induction, carries more stillbirth, low birth weight and prematurity · **pregestational diabetes** (type 1 or 2) — stillbirth, neonatal death, large infants, and 3–4× the malformation rate, "as high as 80 %" with long-standing disease; NTDs, heart defects, caudal dysgenesis; glucose is the agent, insulin is not teratogenic; severity and duration correlate; strict control from before conception brings the rate near normal · **gestational diabetes** — less clear, probably because its onset is after the critical window, and some cases were undiagnosed pregestational diabetes · **PKU** — intellectual disability, microcephaly, cardiac defects unless the low-phenylalanine diet is kept from before conception · **nutrition** — iodine deficiency → endemic cretinism; methyl-deficient diets alter imprinted-gene expression; poor nutrition → low birth weight and defects; severe starvation in pregnancy → a 2–3× rise in schizophrenia in the offspring · **obesity** — over a third of US women of reproductive age obese (BMI > 30) in 2007–08; prepregnancy obesity doubles the NTD risk and raises heart defects, omphalocele and multiple anomalies; mechanism unsettled (glucose, insulin) · **hypoxia** — teratogenic in animals; in humans, high-altitude and cyanotic-heart-disease babies are small but not more malformed · **male-mediated** — chemicals and radiation mutate male germ cells; paternal exposure to mercury, lead, solvents, alcohol and smoking is linked to abortion, low birth weight and defects; **advanced paternal age** raises some structural defects, Down syndrome and new autosomal-dominant mutations, and fathers pass on more new mutations than mothers, with the father's age the dominant factor; even seminal fluid and chemicals carried home on work clothes can transmit exposure | `[E09.01 · R05bz, R05ca]` | ✔ · ✔✔ (obesity: R05bz, meta-analysis — NTD OR 1.87, spina bifida OR 2.24, plus cardiovascular, septal, cleft palate, cleft lip and palate, anorectal atresia, hydrocephaly, limb reduction; | E09.02.01 Prenatal screening (B) · AN81.1 | The perinatologist's set is ultrasound, maternal serum screening, amniocentesis and CVS, together aimed at malformations, genetic abnormalities, growth and pregnancy complications; the stated goal is to give a woman at risk the information to make her own choices — and in-utero therapy has made **the fetus a patient** · **ultrasound**: high-frequency sound, transabdominal or transvaginal (higher resolution), developed in the 1950s, now resolving blood flow in major vessels, heart-valve movement and fluid flow in trachea and bronchi; safe and routine, ~80 % of US pregnant women have at least one scan; reads fetal age and growth, anomalies, amniotic fluid volume, placental position, umbilical flow, and multiples; ultrasound-managed low-birth-weight pregnancies had 60 % lower mortality than unscreened ones; age and growth by **CRL in weeks 5–10**, then BPD, head and abdominal circumference and femur length, repeated over time; detects anencephaly, spina bifida, omphalocele, gastroschisis, heart and facial defects · **nuchal translucency** at 11–14 weeks of pregnancy measures the fluid space behind the neck; combined with serum markers and maternal age it gives a Down syndrome risk, which the woman then uses to decide about invasive testing · **maternal serum screening**: **AFP** is made by the fetal liver, peaks ~14 weeks and leaks into maternal serum (MSAFP), rising through the second trimester then falling after 30 weeks; **high** in NTDs, omphalocele, gastroschisis, bladder exstrophy, amniotic band syndrome, sacrococcygeal teratoma and intestinal atresia; **low** in Down syndrome, trisomy 18, sex-chromosome abnormalities and triploidy; adding hCG, unconjugated oestriol and inhibin A improves detection; screening is never a diagnosis, so a positive result needs an invasive test · **noninvasive prenatal screening (NIPS)**: fetal cells cross from 4–6 weeks after conception and can be analysed by FISH and PCR, but are hard to isolate in enough numbers, so the test now used reads **cell-free DNA from apoptosing placental trophoblast**, 5–50 % of the cell-free DNA in maternal plasma, sequenced for whole fetal genomes; better specificity and predictive value for trisomy 13, 18 and 21 than standard screening; still a screen, so a positive result is confirmed by CVS or amniocentesis, though many expect it to replace them | `[E09.02, E09.04 · R05bq, R05bo, R05bp, R05cc]` | ✔ · ✔✔ (combined first-trimester screening: R05bq, FASTER, 38,167 pregnancies — at a 5 % false-positive rate, detection 87 % at 11 weeks, 85 % at 12, 82 % at 13; second-trimester quadruple 81 %; fully integrated 96 %) · ✚ cfDNA performance now measured: pooled sensitivity 99.3 % and specificity 99.9 % for trisomy 21 in a general pregnant population (R05bo), 99.5 % / 100 % on microarray platforms with a 1.1 % test-failure rate (R05bp) · ✚ the book's "5–50 %" is the full range: the **median fetal fraction is ~13.7 %**, falls as maternal weight rises, and is lower in trisomy 18 (11.0 %) and trisomy 13 (8.0 %), which makes those two harder to call (R05cc, 6,993 pregnancies) |

loss risk approaches that of amniocentesis, but there have been signals of **limb-reduction defects, especially of the digits** · **cordocentesis / PUBS**: a needle into the umbilical cord vein under ultrasound guidance from the 14th week; the preferred route for fetal blood, giving fast cytogenetics and the diagnosis of haematological disease; loss rate slightly higher than amniocentesis · all three invasive tests are reserved for **high-risk** pregnancies: maternal age 35+, a previous child or family history of a genetic problem, maternal disease such as diabetes, or an abnormal scan or serum screen | `[E09.02, E09.04 · R05bl, R05bm, R05bn]` | ✔✔ (loss risk: R05bl, systematic review, 63,723 amniocenteses and 13,011 CVS — procedure-related risk 0.30 % (0.11–0.49) for amniocentesis and 0.20 % (CI crosses zero) for CVS, and 0.12 % and −0.11 % when cases and controls carry the same background risk, i.e. the book's "1 in 300–500, maybe less" is right and the honest answer is "lower still"; R05bm is the earlier meta-analysis, 0.11 % and 0.22 %) · ✚ R05bn: in twins the loss rate after amniocentesis (2.4 %) or CVS (2.0 %) does not differ from the background twin loss rate · **✔✔ the limb-reduction signal, now sourced** (R05lq, 75 affected babies: the **earlier** the CVS the **more severe** the defect — median 56 postmenstrual days, ≈ 6 weeks after fertilization, for an absent limb, up to 72 days, ≈ 8 weeks, for a nail-only defect; D-030, both clocks) · **✚ the 10-week boundary** (R05lr, retrospective, 2,759 CVS pregnancies with known outcome: transverse limb-reduction defects **1 in 1,143** overall, ≈ 4× the Italian background, **2.9 per 1,000 at 9 weeks** versus **1.0 per 1,000 at 10 weeks** GA, the authors advising against CVS before 10 weeks — and themselves warning that heavy loss to follow-up and poor data quality may have affected the result; Beyond Langman — More / Tier D only (D-090 d) · **⚑ D-090 (c)** "not usually before 14 weeks", and the book's reason (fluid volume): the randomised trial (R05lt) compared **11+0–12+6 with 15+0–16+6 weeks GA** — early amniocentesis lost more pregnancies (7.6 % vs 5.9 %) and caused far more talipes (1.3 % vs 0.1 %) — and the Swedish cohort (R05ls, women 35–49) found more musculoskeletal deformity in the amniocentesis group overall (**OR 1.32, 1.11–1.57**, the authors noting the excess was concentrated **before 14 weeks**) and more neonatal respiratory problems (**OR 1.12, 1.02–1.24**, greatest at 14 and 15 weeks). **No trial compares 14 with 15 weeks**, and neither source states a 15-week cut-off, so what is Tier 1 here is that **early amniocentesis is clearly worse than mid-trimester and the operative reason is loss and talipes, not fluid volume** |
| E09.03.01 Fetal therapy (C) | **Transfusion** for fetal anaemia (maternal antibodies or other cause) — needle into the umbilical vein under ultrasound · **medical treatment** for infection, fetal arrhythmia, thyroid problems: usually given to the mother and crossing the placenta, sometimes injected into the fetal gluteal muscle or umbilical vein directly · **surgery**, in two forms, only in trained centres and only when nothing else will do, because mother, infant and later pregnancies are at risk: **open** (uterus opened; done at 18–30 weeks of pregnancy; for neural tube defects, heart abnormalities, diaphragmatic hernia) — for NTDs it reduced shunt dependence and hindbrain herniation and improved motor skill and walking, with **preterm labour the main risk** and overall mortality 6 %; and **fetoscopic** (small incisions, fewer risks) — shunts for urinary obstruction, releasing amniotic bands, ablating the abnormal vessels of twin transfusion syndrome, repairing diaphragmatic hernia · **stem cells and gene therapy**: the fetus has no immunocompetence before 18 weeks, so tissue or cells might be transplanted before then without rejection — research is on haematopoietic stem cells for immunodeficiency and haematological disease, and on gene therapy for Tay-Sachs and cystic fibrosis | `[E09.03, E09.04 · R05br]` | ✔✔ (open NTD repair: R05br, MOMS, randomised, stopped early for efficacy — shunt placement 40 % vs 82 % with postnatal repair, better mental-development and motor score at 30 months, less hindbrain herniation, more walking, at the cost of preterm delivery and uterine dehiscence) |

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decisions D-057 (owner picks) · D-090 added by X2a, 22 Nov 2026: (a)–(c) owner picks, (d)–(f) notes

The book's range is the older passive-surveillance figure; active ascertainment finds several times more. Options in D-057.
- **(b) How often Zika infection in pregnancy harms the fetus** — book: ~30 % of pregnancies in which the mother tests positive are adversely affected, worst at 7–12 weeks, abnormalities up to 18 weeks. R05bw (US Zika Pregnancy Registry, 972 completed pregnancies): Zika-associated birth defects in **5 %** with laboratory evidence of possible recent infection, **10 %** with confirmed infection, **15 %** with confirmed first-trimester infection. The book's 30 % probably counts any adverse outcome (including loss and non-structural findings) in a first-trimester-weighted cohort; the registry counts birth defects. The trimester gradient agrees in both. **X2a (22 Nov 2026) — R05bw full text read (PMC), which settles the comparison:** the registry's denominator is *completed pregnancies* (live births **and** losses, any gestational age) with laboratory evidence of possible recent infection, and its numerator counts **only structural defects**, in two mutually exclusive groups — brain abnormalities and/or microcephaly (43 of the 51 cases), and NTDs/other early brain malformations, eye abnormalities or other consequences of CNS dysfunction without a brain abnormality. A pregnancy loss on its own, and any non-structural finding, is **not** counted. ## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Weeks 1–2 are safe — it's all or none."** The book itself corrects this: axis specification starts at the blastocyst late in week 1, so cranial–caudal and left–right errors there can give almost any defect; the all-or-none rule holds for many insults but not for this `[E09.01]` ✔.
2. **"Weeks 3–8 are the only time defects can be caused."** They carry most *structural* defects, but axis-stage exposures do too, and after week 8 the brain keeps differentiating, so function can still be damaged. No stage is completely safe `[E09.01, E09.04]` ✔.
3. **"One organ has one sensitive window."** Cleft palate alone can be induced at the blastocyst, at gastrulation, at the early limb bud and when the shelves form `[E09.01]` ✔.
4. **"Malformation, disruption and deformation are the same thing."** A malformation forms wrongly; a disruption destroys something already formed (amniotic bands); a deformation moulds it mechanically over time (clubfoot) and is often reversible after birth `[E09.01]` ✔.
5. **"A syndrome and an association are both diagnoses."** A syndrome has a known common cause, so recurrence risk is known; an association is a non-random cluster with no cause found — useful because finding one component prompts a search for the rest `[E09.01]` ✔.
6. **"Minor anomalies don't matter."** They are harmless in themselves but flag major defects: one minor anomaly 3 %, two 10 %, three or more 20 %; ear anomalies are the strongest clue `[E09.01 · R05lp]` ✔✔ for the three-or-more step (19.6 % in 4,305 newborns) · ✔ for the one- and two-anomaly figures. **Teach with it:** the 90 % figure still quoted in older notes came from earlier work of a different design (Marden et al. 1964, as popularised) and was corrected by this one — a clean example of why a number gets re-measured (✚, beyond Langman — More / Tier D only).
7. **"Most birth defects have a known cause."** ~55 % are multifactorial or of unknown origin; only ~15 % are purely environmental and ~30 % purely genetic `[E09.01]` ✔.
8. **"Birth defects are commoner in some ethnic groups."** The book states the frequencies are the same across the groups it lists `[E09.01]` ✔.
9. **"Vaccines in pregnancy cause birth defects."** There is no evidence that immunisation against measles, mumps, hepatitis, polio or influenza harms the fetus — and rubella **vaccination** is what nearly eliminated congenital rubella syndrome `[E09.01]` ✔.
10. **"A mother with no symptoms means a safe infection."** CMV and toxoplasma frequently leave the mother well and the fetus damaged, sometimes only declaring itself months later; in the mercury poisonings the mothers were often unaffected `[E09.01 · R05cb]` ✔✔.
11. **"The teratogen is the drug, so a fever is harmless."** Hyperthermia from fever, a hot tub or a sauna is teratogenic in its own right and hits neurulation `[E09.01]` ✔.
12. **"Thalidomide is a historical problem."** It is still prescribed (leprosy, lupus, graft-versus-host disease, AIDS) and still causes limb and other defects `[E09.01]` ✔.
13. **"If a drug is needed for a serious disease, stopping it is the safe choice."** For seizure disorders the drug prevents seizures, so the decision is about which drug and what dose, not whether `[E09.01 · R05bu]` ✔✔.
14. **"Insulin is the teratogen in diabetic pregnancy."** The evidence points to glucose; insulin is not teratogenic, and control from before conception brings the rate near normal `[E09.01]` ✔.
15. **"Gestational diabetes carries the same defect risk as pregestational."** Its onset is after the critical window; the less clear signal may partly be undiagnosed pregestational diabetes `[E09.01]` ✔.
16. **"Only the mother's exposures matter."** Paternal chemical and radiation exposure, smoking and advanced paternal age are all linked to defects and new mutations, and the father's age is the dominant factor in how many new mutations a child carries `[E09.01]` ✔.
17. **"A positive screening test is a diagnosis."** Serum screening, nuchal translucency and cfDNA are screens; a positive result is confirmed by amniocentesis or CVS `[E09.02, E09.04]` ✔.
18. **"Cell-free fetal DNA is DNA from fetal cells."** It is cell-free DNA from dying placental **trophoblast**, not from fetal cells — fetal cells themselves are present but too few to use `[E09.02]` ✔ · ✚ which is also why a confined placental abnormality can give a false positive.
19. **"Amniocentesis carries a 1-in-100 risk of losing the pregnancy."** The old quoted figures are too high: the book already says 1 in 300–500 or less, and pooled data put the procedure-related risk near 0.1–0.3 %, and close to zero when compared with women of the same background risk `[E09.02 · R05bl]` ✔✔.
20. **"CVS results take longer because the sample is smaller."** CVS gives results in 2–3 days *because* the cell yield is large; amniocentesis takes 1–2 weeks because shed fetal cells divide slowly and must be cultured `[E09.02]` ✔.
21. **"High AFP means a neural tube defect."** It rises in several defects where tissue leaks (omphalocele, gastroschisis, bladder exstrophy, amniotic bands, sacrococcygeal teratoma, intestinal atresia) and **falls** in Down syndrome, trisomy 18, sex-chromosome abnormalities and triploidy `[E09.02]` ✔.
22. **"Fetal surgery is a cure with no cost."** For neural tube defects it reduced shunting and improved motor outcome, but at the price of preterm delivery and uterine dehiscence, with mortality around 6 % `[E09.03 · R05br]` ✔✔.
23. **"Prenatal diagnosis is about finding defects."** The book states the goal as giving a woman at risk the information to make her own informed choice — and in-utero therapy has turned the fetus into a patient `[E09.02]` ✔.
24. **"Prevention can start at the first prenatal visit."** Most defects are induced before week 8 after fertilization, and most women first attend around then — which is why every prevention strategy has to begin before conception `[E09.01, E09.01 CC]` ✔.

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
Two clocks run at once and the chapter switches between them without saying so; the five words for defect types sound interchangeable; and the screening tests arrive as a list of acronyms with no shape. → Teach the two-clock strip first and label every number with its clock (the single highest-yield fix in this chapter); teach the defect words as **one picture each** — formed wrong, destroyed, squashed, known cause, unexplained cluster (shape before names, D1); lay the tests on the two-clock timeline in the order a pregnancy meets them (NT 11–14 → CVS → serum/quad → amniocentesis → cordocentesis, with cfDNA alongside from 10 weeks) so the acronyms get positions rather than definitions; then one branching card: **screen → positive → confirm**, which is the exam point and misconception 17. ≤ 3 new terms per beat (D2); every clinical beat stays humane and diagram-led (D6).
