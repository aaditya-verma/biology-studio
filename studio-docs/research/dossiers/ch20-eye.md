## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E20.01.01 Optic cup and lens vesicle · AN43.4, AN41.1
- **The eye is brain plus skin.** The retina, its pigment layer and the optic nerve grow out of the **forebrain wall**; the lens (and the front layer of the cornea) come from the **surface ectoderm** over it. `[E20.01, E20.08]` **✔** · links **D-079** (forebrain, Ch 18)
- **Groove → vesicle → cup.** Around day 22 a groove on each side of the forebrain fold bulges out as the **optic vesicle** when the tube closes; the vesicle meets the surface, then folds in on itself into a **double-walled cup** joined to the brain by the **optic stalk**. `[E20.01 · R06]` **✔** (R06 stage features; full text §10)
- **Each layer talks to the other.** The vesicle makes the surface thicken into the **lens placode**, which sinks as a pit and pinches off as the **lens vesicle** in week 5; the book goes further: with no lens placode, the vesicle does not fold into a cup at all. `[E20.01, E20.07 · R05kj]` **✔** (⚑ b on PAX6's part) · *mouse*: with PAX6 removed only from the lens ectoderm, no lens forms and the retina still differentiates — but as **several** separate retinas in one vesicle, not one cup (R05kj; mechanism, ⚑ b)
- **The gap underneath.** The fold also takes in the cup's lower side, leaving a **choroid (optic) fissure** along cup and stalk; the **hyaloid artery** runs in through it. The fissure's lips **fuse in week 7**, turning the cup's rim into the round pupil. `[E20.01, E20.06 · R05kc]` **✔✔** (*human*: edges fusing at days 41–44) · ✚ the edge cells briefly loosen and rearrange; no apoptosis seen (R05kc)
- **Why it matters:** a fissure that does not close leaves a **coloboma** — a gap at the bottom of the iris and sometimes deeper (§4, ⚑ a). `[E20.07 CC]` **✔**

### E20.02.01 Retina · AN41.1, AN43.2
- **Two walls, two jobs.** The cup's **outer wall** becomes the thin **pigment layer**; the **inner wall** becomes the thick **neural retina**. The space between them (**intraretinal space**) closes as the two walls come to lie against each other. `[E20.01, E20.02]` **✔** · ✚ the clinical link (More only, beyond Langman): a **retinal detachment** is the neural retina separating from the retinal pigment epithelium `[R05mz]` — which is the plane where the two cup walls met `[E20.01, E20.02]`; misconception 1
- **Built like the brain.** The back four-fifths (**pars optica**) layers itself as the brain wall does: cells next to the old space become **rods and cones**; a **mantle layer** gives the **outer and inner nuclear layers** and the **ganglion cell layer**; on the inner surface a **fibre layer** carries ganglion-cell axons to the stalk. `[E20.02]` **✔**
- **Light goes the long way.** Because the cup folded in, the light-sensing cells face **away** from the light: light crosses the other layers first. `[E20.02]` **✔**
- **Rods far outnumber cones.** Book: ~**120 million** rods, ~**6–7 million** cones; rods are more sensitive, cones see colour. `[E20.02 · R05kg]` **✔✔** — *human* whole retinas: ~**92 million** rods and ~**4.6 million** cones (R05kg; within ~1.4×, note d)
- **The fovea is finished after birth.** ✚ The central cone-only spot is recognisable at fetal week 20–22, gets its pit at week 25 and reaches its adult shape only at ~**15 months** after birth `[E20.02 · R05kh]` (More only, beyond Langman)
- **The front fifth stays thin.** The **pars caeca** (one cell layer) lines the back of the **iris** and the **ciliary body** — continuous with the retina. `[E20.02]` **✔**

**The eye field didn't split or didn't grow (anophthalmia, microphthalmia, cyclopia).**
- **Microphthalmia:** a small eye, possibly only about **two-thirds** of normal volume, usually with other eye defects; causes include infections in pregnancy (**CMV, toxoplasmosis**). **Anophthalmia:** no eye — sometimes a trace of eye tissue — usually with severe skull and brain defects. `[E20.07 CC · R05kl]` ✔✔ often syndromic / with other defects (R05kl) · ✔ the rest · ✚ maternal diabetes about doubles the rate of **nonsyndromic** cases (R05kl) · links **D-057** (CMV, diabetes — Ch 9)
- **PAX6:** mutations → **aniridia**; may also add to small or absent eyes. `[E20.07 CC · R05ki, R05kq]` ✔✔ · ✚ dose: a child with both copies faulty had no eyes and brain defects (R05ki, More only)
- **Cyclopia / synophthalmia:** one eye or two fused eyes after **midline loss** at the prechordal-plate stage (~days 19–21) or during early face building, which leaves the eye field unsplit; always with **holoprosencephaly**. Risks: alcohol, maternal diabetes, **SHH** mutations, cholesterol-pathway faults that block SHH signalling. `[E20.07 CC]` ✔ · links **D-079** (SHH, cholesterol, holoprosencephaly — Ch 18), **D-078** (holoprosencephaly face — Ch 17) — no new ⚑

**The cup's seam didn't close (coloboma).**
- **Coloboma** = a gap left where the **choroid fissure** failed to fuse in week 7 — at the **bottom** of the eye, because that is where the fissure is. It may be in the iris only or run back into ciliary body, retina, choroid and optic nerve; it often comes with other eye defects. **PAX2** faults cause optic-nerve colobomas and, with kidney defects, the **renal coloboma syndrome** (links Ch 16). Eyelid colobomas also occur. `[E20.07 CC · R05km, R05kn]` ✔ often with other eye defects · ✚ 58 % had another eye disorder over follow-up, mostly amblyopia or strabismus (R05km) · **⚑ a** "usually in the iris only" · ✚ CHARGE in 1 in 8 (R05km) · links **D-076** (Ch 16 kidney)
- **Problem 3** (both irises cleft below) → coloboma from failed fissure closure; check the retina, choroid and optic nerve too. `[E20.09]` ✔

**The lens didn't form or went cloudy.**
- **Congenital cataract** — the lens turns opaque before birth; usually **genetic**; **rubella** in the **4th–7th weeks** damaged the lens, while infection after week 7 spared the lens but could still cause hearing loss (cochlea). `[E20.07 CC · R05ko]` ✔✔ mostly hereditary (R05ko) · ✔ the rubella window (no human window study read; note g) · links **D-057** (rubella — Ch 9), **D-082** (rubella and the ear — Ch 19)
- **Aphakia** (no lens) and **aniridia** (no iris) — rare; faulty induction of their source tissues. `[E20.07 CC]` ✔ · Problem 1 (one-sided aphakia → the lens placode was not induced or did not form) `[E20.09]` ✔
- **Thalidomide** — the earliest-exposure cases carry the eye findings (eye size and shape: axial length, refraction). `[— · R05cd]` link **D-067** (thalidomide window, Ch 12) — no new ⚑

**The front of the eye didn't open or drain.**
- **Persistent iridopupillary membrane** — the sheet in front of the lens stays instead of clearing as the anterior chamber forms. `[E20.07 CC]` ✔
- **Glaucoma** — blocked outflow at the canal of Schlemm raises eye pressure; in **primary congenital glaucoma** the angle itself forms abnormally. `[E20.04 · R05kp]` ✔ the drainage line · ✚ congenital rate and ethnic risk (R05kp)

**The vessels that should vanish didn't.**

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decision D-085 (owner picks)
- **(a) Where a coloboma usually is.** Book: the cleft is **usually in the iris only**, though it may run back into ciliary body, retina, choroid and optic nerve. *Human*: in a population cohort of children (R05km, 33 cases), only **36 %** were front-of-eye only — **39 %** were back-of-eye only and **24 %** both, so about **two in three** reached the back of the eye. A registry case-control study of **isolated** cases (R05kn, 46 cases) found **40 of 46** in the iris — closer to the book, but it set out to study mainly iris coloboma, so it leans toward the iris. Also, R05km's "anterior segment only" is not the same as "iris only". Options: (1) default (book line); (2) card: "the gap can be in the iris, the back of the eye, or both — a visible iris gap means the back of the eye must be checked", the book line and both figures in More; (3) card keeps "often in the iris" and adds the back-of-eye share. *Recommended (2)* — it matches Problem 3's own lesson (look deeper) and doesn't depend on which cohort is right.
- **(b) Does the eye need PAX6 to make its cup?** Book: PAX6 marks the eye field, but **seems not to be needed for the optic cup itself**; the cup is steered by signals between vesicle, mesenchyme and lens ectoderm, and with no lens placode no cup forms. *Tier 1*: complete PAX6 loss **stops the optic vesicle developing** in humans and mice (R05kj, abstract); a *human* child with both PAX6 copies faulty had **no eyes** (R05ki); *mouse* lens-ectoderm-only loss → no lens and several retinas in one vesicle instead of one cup (R05kj). So PAX6 is needed for the eye to get past the vesicle — whether directly in the cup or only through the lens is the open part. Options: (1) default (book line); (2) card: "PAX6 is the eye's master switch — without it there is no lens and the vesicle never becomes a proper cup; one faulty copy → aniridia", the book's "not needed for the cup" in More with the newer evidence; (3) drop PAX6's role in the cup from cards and keep only the lens and aniridia lines. *Recommended (2)* — the exam answer (PAX6 = master gene, aniridia) stays and nothing false is taught.

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"A retinal detachment pulls the retina off the choroid."** (map row) It splits the **neural retina from its own pigment layer** — the two walls of the optic cup, whose gap (intraretinal space) closed as they met `[E20.01, E20.02 · R05mz]` ✔ the two walls · ✚ the detachment plane (neural retina off the pigment epithelium, R05mz).
2. **"The iris muscles are mesoderm, like other muscle."** (map row) Sphincter and dilator pupillae grow from the **optic cup's ectoderm** `[E20.02]` ✔.
3. **"The eye grows from the skin."** The retina and optic nerve are **brain** (forebrain wall); of the eyeball's own tissues, only the lens and the front corneal layer are surface ectoderm `[E20.01]` ✔.
4. **"The lens is part of the retina's cup."** It comes from a separate **placode** of surface ectoderm that the vesicle induces `[E20.01 · R05kj]` ✔ (*mouse* mechanism).
5. **"The optic nerve is a peripheral nerve."** It is a **brain tract**: glia from the stalk wall, wrapped in pia-arachnoid and dura `[E20.06]` ✔.
6. **"The rods and cones face the light."** They face **away**; light crosses the other layers first, a result of the cup folding in `[E20.02]` ✔.
7. **"Cones outnumber rods."** Rods outnumber cones about **20 to 1** `[E20.02 · R05kg]` ✔✔.
8. **"A coloboma is a hole anywhere in the iris."** It is a gap at the **bottom**, where the fissure was — and it can reach the back of the eye `[E20.07 CC · R05km]` ✔ · ⚑ a.
9. **"The hyaloid artery disappears completely."** Its stem stays as the **central artery of the retina**; only the part in the vitreous goes `[E20.05, E20.06]` ✔.
10. **"The lens stops growing at birth."** New fibres are added **for life** `[E20.03]` ✔.
11. **"The choroid and sclera are new tissues unique to the eye."** They match the brain's **pia** and **dura** and are continuous with the nerve's sheaths `[E20.04]` ✔.
12. **"Two eyes start as two separate fields."** There is **one** field across the midline; **SHH** splits it — fail to split → cyclopia `[E20.07]` ✔.
13. **"PAX6 only matters for the lens."** PAX6 mutations cause **aniridia** and can add to absent eyes `[E20.07 CC · R05ki]` ✔✔ · ✚ two faulty copies → no eyes and brain defects (R05ki).
14. **"A baby's eye is a small adult eye."** The **fovea** matures only at ~15 months after birth (✚) and the lens keeps growing `[E20.02, E20.03 · R05kh]` ✔ · ✚.
15. **"Fetal eyes are open."** The lids **fuse** in week 8–9 and part only around **week 20** after fertilization `[— · R05kd]` ✚ (More only, beyond Langman).
16. **"An aniridia baby just has an eye problem."** A deletion that also takes **WT1** brings a Wilms tumour risk (WAGR) — check the kidneys `[E20.07 CC · R05kq]` ✚ (More only).
17. **"All congenital cataracts are from rubella."** Most are **hereditary**; rubella matters in weeks 4–7 `[E20.07 CC · R05ko]` ✔✔.

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
- **Two sources, one organ** — teach the eye as **brain (cup, nerve) · skin (lens, corneal surface) · mesenchyme/crest (coats, stroma)** before any part name; one colour per source.
- **The fold that flips the retina** — draw the vesicle folding in before naming layers; the backwards retina and the detachment seam both follow from it.
- **The fissure** — one gap explains three things: how vessels get in, where colobomas are, and why they are at the bottom.
- **Molecular alphabet** — PAX6 / SHH / PAX2 / FGF / TGF-β / MITF / CHX10 / SOX2 / L-MAF: teach only "one field, SHH splits it; front skin makes lens, PAX6 dose matters" at Tier A–B; the rest is Tier D.
- **Clocks:** all embryonic events are after fertilization, including the book's "gestation" lines (note h); fetal papers often give no clock (note i).
