**Fetal-size standard (X2a):** the Tier 1 reference set for this chapter is INTERGROWTH-21st — **R05li** for CRL dating (9–14 weeks GA), **R05lj** for growth from 14 to 42 weeks GA (head and abdominal circumference, BPD, OFD, femur length), **R05lk** for newborn size. ## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E08.01.03 Time of birth · AN80.6
- Birth comes about **266 days (38 weeks) after fertilization**; obstetricians count **280 days (40 weeks) counted from the start of the last period** (LNMP). `[E08.01, E08.10 · R05ap]` **✔✔** (R05ap: median 268 d from ovulation)
- Fertilization normally happens within half a day of ovulation, but sperm can wait up to ~6 days, so conception follows sex in a 6-day window ending on ovulation day — one reason the fertilization date is rarely known. `[E08.01 · R05aq]` **✔✔** (R05aq: 221 women, daily hormones: conception only from a 6-day window ending on ovulation)
- The LMP rule works well with regular 28-day cycles; irregular cycles and implantation bleeding (~day 14 after fertilization, mistaken for a period) cause errors. `[E08.01]` **✔** · ✚ even timed exactly from ovulation, natural pregnancy length varies by ~5 weeks `[R05ap]` **✚**
- Most babies arrive within 10–14 days of the due date; much earlier = premature, much later = postmature. `[E08.01]` **✔**
- Age can be estimated from LMP + length, weight and form; ultrasound CRL is the tool in weeks 7–14 (book: accurate to 1–2 days), then BPD, head and abdominal circumference and femur length (weeks 16–30). `[E08.01 · R05ar, R05as]` **✔** (method) · **⚑ D-044 b** (accuracy: R05ar ±4.7 d for one scan, ±2.7 d for three)

### E08.03.01 Chorion frondosum and laeve; deciduae · AN80.1, AN80.3
- Early on, villi cover the whole chorion; those at the **embryonic pole** keep growing (**chorion frondosum**, "bushy"), those at the **abembryonic pole** degenerate, so by month 3 that side is smooth (**chorion laeve**). `[E08.03]` **✔**
- The **decidua** is the endometrium's functional layer (shed at birth). Over the frondosum it is the **decidua basalis** (compact layer of large, lipid- and glycogen-rich decidual cells = the decidual plate, tightly bound to the chorion); over the abembryonic pole it is the **decidua capsularis**; the rest of the uterine lining is the **decidua parietalis**. `[E08.03]` **✔**
- As the chorionic sac grows, the capsularis stretches, degenerates, and the chorion laeve meets and fuses with the decidua parietalis — the uterine cavity disappears. `[E08.03]` **✔**
- Only the frondosum exchanges; **chorion frondosum + decidua basalis = the placenta**. `[E08.03, E08.10]` **✔**
- The amnion fuses with the chorion into the **amniochorionic membrane**, which closes the chorionic cavity; this is the membrane that ruptures in labour ("waters breaking"). `[E08.03, E08.05]` **✔**

### E08.04.01 Structure of the placenta · AN80.3
- From month 4 the placenta has a **fetal part** (chorion frondosum, bordered by the chorionic plate) and a **maternal part** (decidua basalis; its decidual plate is the layer most firmly built into the placenta). `[E08.04, E08.10]` **✔**
- In the junctional zone trophoblast and decidual cells mix (decidual + syncytial giant cells, lots of amorphous matrix); most cytotrophoblast has gone by now. `[E08.04]` **✔**
- Between the chorionic and decidual plates lie the **intervillous spaces**, full of **maternal blood**; they come from syncytiotrophoblast lacunae and are lined by fetal syncytium. Villous trees grow into these blood lakes. `[E08.04]` **✔**
- In months 4–5 **decidual septa** (maternal core, syncytial cover) jut into the spaces but don't reach the chorionic plate, dividing the placenta into **cotyledons** that still connect. `[E08.04]` **✔**
- The placenta covers ~15–30 % of the uterine wall; it thickens by **branching of villi**, not by deeper invasion. `[E08.04]` **✔**
- Maternal blood arrives through **80–100 spiral arteries** that spurt it deep into the spaces; it drains back through **endometrial veins**. A mature placenta holds ~150 mL of maternal blood, refreshed 3–4 times a minute, over 4–14 m² of villi. `[E08.04, E08.10]` **✔**
- Getting there needs **endovascular cytotrophoblast**: cells from anchoring-villus tips invade the spiral artery ends, replace their endothelium (an epithelial-to-endothelial switch) and turn narrow high-resistance vessels into wide low-resistance ones. `[E08.02 · R05az]` **✔✔** (R05az: invading human cytotrophoblasts switch to a vascular adhesion phenotype; it fails in preeclampsia)
- At term it is a disc 15–25 cm across, ~3 cm thick, ~500–600 g, with 15–20 cotyledons visible on the maternal side; the fetal side is chorionic plate covered by amnion, with chorionic vessels converging on the cord. `[E08.04]` **✔**

### E08.04.02 The placental membrane · AN80.3
- Maternal and fetal blood are **separated** by the placental membrane; normally they don't mix, though a few fetal cells leak through tiny breaks. `[E08.04]` **✔** (R05bd implies it via Rh prophylaxis; a fetomaternal-haemorrhage source would raise it to ✔✔)
- At first it has **4 layers**: fetal endothelium, villus connective tissue, cytotrophoblast, syncytium. From **month 4** many villi lose cytotrophoblast and core tissue, leaving **syncytium + endothelium** — faster exchange. `[E08.02, E08.04]` **✔**
- Because maternal blood touches a chorion-derived layer directly, the human placenta is **haemochorial**. `[E08.04, E08.10]` **✔**
- It is **not a true barrier**: gases move by simple diffusion (fetal O₂ intake at term 20–30 mL/min; supply depends on blood flow); nutrients and electrolytes cross readily. `[E08.04]` **✔**
- **Maternal IgG** crosses (book: from ~14 weeks — book clock, after fertilization, ≈ 16 weeks LMP), giving passive immunity; adult IgG levels only by age 3. `[E08.04 · R05bb, R05bc]` **✔✔** (R05bb: fetal IgG rises from early in the second trimester, most in the third; R05bc: IgG is the only class that crosses, via FcRn on syncytiotrophoblast — ✚ mechanism)
- Steroid hormones cross easily, thyroxine slowly; synthetic progestins can masculinize female fetuses; DES caused vaginal clear-cell carcinoma and reproductive-tract defects; many viruses (rubella, CMV, varicella, measles, polio…) and most drugs cross; maternal heroin or cocaine can cause fetal dependence. `[E08.04 CC]` **✔**

### E08.05.01 Umbilical cord · AN80.2, AN80.7
- In week 5 three things pass the **primitive umbilical ring** (the amnion–ectoderm junction): the connecting stalk (allantois + 2 umbilical arteries + 1 vein), the yolk stalk (vitelline duct) with its vessels, and the canal joining intra- and extraembryonic cavities. `[E08.05]` **✔**
- The amnion expands, wraps both stalks together and forms the **primitive cord**; at the end of month 3 it reaches the chorion and the chorionic cavity closes. `[E08.05]` **✔**
- Gut loops lie in the cord as a normal **physiological umbilical hernia** (from week 6) and return by about the end of month 3 (book Ch 8: by week 12). `[E08.01, E08.05]` **✔**
- When the allantois, vitelline duct and its vessels are gone, the cord holds **2 arteries + 1 vein** in **Wharton jelly** (proteoglycan-rich, protective); the arteries' muscular, elastic walls clamp down fast after the cord is tied. `[E08.05, E08.10]` **✔**
- At birth the cord is ~1–2 cm thick and 50–60 cm long, often twisted (false knots); length reflects fetal movement. `[E08.07 CC]` **✔**
- Insertion is usually eccentric, sometimes marginal, rarely into the membranes outside the placenta (**velamentous**). `[E08.04]` **✔**
- **Single umbilical artery:** ~1 in 200 newborns; the book gives ~20 % with heart or other vascular defects. `[E08.07 CC · R05bf]` **✔✔** (incidence: R05bf 0.55 % of live-born) · **✔** (book: ~20 % heart/vascular) · ✚ R05bf: 27 % of live-born SUA infants have some malformation; 16 % of isolated SUA have a renal anomaly (mostly minor) · ✚ R05bg: isolated SUA carries no extra aneuploidy risk

## 5. ⚑ Watch items → decision D-044 (owner picks)
- **(a) When the placenta takes over progesterone** — book: by the end of month 4 (≈ 16 wk after fertilization, ≈ 18 wk LMP) the placenta makes enough to keep the pregnancy without the corpus luteum. R05ba (43 normal pregnancies, serum): the placental share (progesterone vs the ovarian marker 17-OH-progesterone) rises between ~4 and 9 weeks — the paper's clock is unclear, and it shows a rising share, not that the placenta is already sufficient. Lutectomy studies (Csapo 1972–73) are the classic evidence for an earlier hand-over but have no PubMed abstract — check the full text before adding them (≈).
- **(b) How accurate CRL dating is** — book: ultrasound measures CRL accurately (1–2 days) in weeks 7–14 — this may mean measurement precision rather than dating error. R05ar (menstrual age, 6–14 wk): a single CRL dates the pregnancy within ±4.7 days (95 %), three measurements ±2.7 days; R05as (known insemination date): −7 to +4 days.
- **(c) Share of twins that are dizygotic** — the book says ~90 % (E08.08) and two-thirds (E08.10 Summary). Arithmetic: the book's own US twin rate (32.6 / 1,000) with MZ 3–4 / 1,000 gives DZ ≈ 89 %; R05bj (twins ~13 / 1,000 maternities on average, MZ rate near-constant ~3.5) gives DZ ≈ 73 %. So the 90 % fits a high-twinning, ART-heavy population; two-thirds to three-quarters fits spontaneous twins worldwide.
- **(e) Amniotic fluid volume after 20 weeks** — book: rises from ~450 mL (20 wk) to 800–1,000 mL (37 wk). R05be (705 normal measurements, gestational weeks): no significant change in the mean from 22 to 39 wk (~780 mL; 95 % range ~300–2,000 mL), contrary to the expected rise.
- **(d) K1 note, not a ⚑:** concept E08.04.05 "Placenta accreta and previa" sits in Ch 8, but Langman 14e covers **previa only, in Ch 4** (E04.04) and has **no accreta**. Options in D-044 d.
Until decided: cards, Checks and scenes give the **book answer**; More carries "⚑ Newer evidence (D-044)" lines; on-screen ages stay book ages.

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Pregnancy is 40 weeks from conception."** 40 weeks counts from the last period; from fertilization it is ~38 weeks (266 days) `[E08.01 · R05ap]` ✔✔.
2. **"Maternal and fetal blood mix in the placenta."** Fetal blood stays in villus capillaries; maternal blood bathes the villi; a membrane separates them — though a few fetal red cells leak across, which is why Rh disease exists `[E08.04, E08.04 CC]` ✔.
3. **"The placenta is a barrier that protects the baby from drugs and germs."** Most drugs and many viruses cross freely (the book's own Problem 2) `[E08.04 CC]` ✔.
4. **"The placenta is purely fetal."** It is fetal (chorion frondosum) + maternal (decidua basalis) `[E08.03, E08.04]` ✔.
5. **"Umbilical arteries carry oxygen-rich blood (they're arteries)."** They carry the fetus's used blood **to** the placenta; the single vein brings oxygenated blood back `[Ch 13 fetal circulation; Ch 8 gives no flow direction]` ✔.
6. **"The placenta grows thicker by invading deeper."** It thickens by branching of existing villi `[E08.04]` ✔.
7. **"Cotyledons are completely separate compartments."** Septa don't reach the chorionic plate, so the spaces connect `[E08.04]` ✔.
8. **"All identical twins share one placenta."** Splitting at the two-cell stage gives two placentas and chorions — like fraternal twins `[E08.08 · R05bk]` ✔✔.
9. **"Fraternal twins share a placenta."** Each usually has its own; fused placentas still keep separate chorions and amnions `[E08.08]` ✔.
10. **"Gut in the cord at 9 weeks means omphalocele."** It is the normal physiological hernia; it returns by about week 12 after fertilization (book clock) — on a scan (LMP clock) that is ~14 weeks of pregnancy; see the two-clocks note `[E08.01, E08.05]` ✔.
11. **"The mother's antibodies of every class protect the fetus."** Only IgG crosses `[E08.04 · R05bc]` ✔✔.
12. **"Rh disease hits the first Rh+ baby hardest."** Sensitization usually happens at the first delivery; later pregnancies are worse `[E08.04 CC · R05bd]` ✔✔.
13. **"Too much amniotic fluid = the baby drinks too little because it's lazy."** Polyhydramnios often means the fetus **can't** swallow (anencephaly, oesophageal atresia) or maternal diabetes; many are idiopathic `[E08.07 CC · R05ll]` ✔✔ for the diabetes share (23.9 %) · ✔ for the rest (R05ll uses a different definition of polyhydramnios — see §3) — and "idiopathic" is not a clean bill: in that series about 1 in 10 of those babies turned out to have an anomaly after birth (✚, beyond Langman).
14. **"Low birth weight = born too early."** LBW is weight only; a term baby can be LBW (growth restriction) and a preterm baby can be appropriate for age `[E08.01 CC · R05at]` ✔✔.
