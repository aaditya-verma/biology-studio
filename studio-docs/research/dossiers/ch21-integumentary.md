## 2. Tier A facts — none in Ch 21
**No Ch 21 concept is Tier A in K1** (all are B, C or D — `tools/map/concepts.txt`). The two exam-high rows are written to the Tier A standard so cards can lift them straight.

### E21.01.01 Epidermis (B, exam-high) · AN4.2, AN72.1
- **Skin has two sources.** The outer **epidermis** comes from **surface ectoderm**; the inner **dermis** from the **mesenchyme** beneath. `[E21.01]` **✔**
- **One layer becomes four.** A single ectoderm layer gains a flat **periderm** on top early in month 2, then an **intermediate** layer, and by the end of month 4 shows its adult four: **basal (germinative)** — the dividing layer; **spinous**; **granular**; **horny** (dead keratin-filled cells). `[E21.01 · R05kr]` **✔✔** two layers at 8 wk EGA, intermediate layer from 11–21 wk EGA (*human*)
- **The periderm is a temporary coat.** It is shed in the second half of pregnancy into the amniotic fluid as the upper layers start to keratinize. `[E21.01 · R05ku]` **✔✔**
- **Pigment cells are immigrants.** **Melanocytes** come from the **neural crest** and enter the epidermis in the first 3 months; they make melanin in **melanosomes** and hand them along their branches to the **keratinocytes** of skin and hair. `[E21.01 · R05ks]` **✔✔** (*human*: present from 50 days EGA, absent before) · links **D-079** (crest — Ch 18)
- **Fingerprints are basal-layer ridges.** The basal layer folds into ridges and hollows that show on the surface as **fingerprints**; their patterns are inherited and used in genetics and forensics. `[E21.01, E21.01 CC · R05kv]` **✔** · ✚ ridges first appear at **10 weeks EGA** at two foci on the fingertip and spread outward, after nerves have arrived (R05kv, More only, beyond Langman; note g)
- **Why it matters:** a fault in the horny layer → **ichthyosis** (§4, ⚑ b); a fault in crest pigment cells → **albinism, piebaldism, Waardenburg** (§4). `[E21.01 CC]` **✔**

### E21.01.02 Dermis (B) · AN4.1, AN4.2
- **Three sources, by region.** The dermis comes from **lateral plate mesoderm** in the **limbs and body wall**, from **paraxial mesoderm (dermatomes of the somites)** in the **back**, and from **neural crest** in the **face and neck**. `[E21.01, E21.06]` **✔** · links **D-066** (dermomyotome and dermis — Ch 11), **D-078** (crest of the face — Ch 17) — no new ⚑
- **Papillae and fat.** In months 3–4 the upper dermis (**corium**) pushes **dermal papillae** up into the epidermis, many with a capillary or a sensory nerve ending; the deep **subcorium** fills with fat. `[E21.01]` **✔**
- **Vernix.** At birth a whitish paste — **vernix caseosa**, from sebaceous secretions plus shed epidermal cells and hairs — protects the skin from soaking in amniotic fluid. `[E21.01]` **✔**

**The horny layer (keratinization).**
- **Ichthyosis** = too much keratin, so the skin scales; a group of **inherited** disorders. Book: usually **autosomal recessive**, sometimes **X-linked**; the most severe newborn form is the **harlequin fetus** (thick plates split by deep cracks). `[E21.01 CC · R05le, R05lf, R05kz]` ✔ inherited · ✔ harlequin · ✚ harlequin is recessive, ABCA12 (R05lf) · **⚑ b** "usually autosomal recessive" — the commonest is FLG-type vulgaris (~1 in 250, semidominant, R05le), then X-linked (1 in 1,500 males, R05kz); a teratogen link (systemic **retinoids** — skin and other defects) is **D-057**, no new ⚑.

**The pigment cells (neural crest).**
- **Albinism** — melanocytes are present but make little or no **melanin**: skin, hair and eyes (OCA). `[E21.01 CC · R05la]` ✔ · ✚ ≥ 1 in 14,000; TYR the commonest gene (R05la)
- **Piebaldism** and **Waardenburg syndrome (WS)** — **white patches** of skin and hair (a white forelock), from crest cells that failed to **migrate or multiply**; WS adds different-coloured irises and **deafness** (no crest melanocytes in the cochlea's stria vascularis); some WS from **PAX3** mutations (types 1 and 3). `[E21.01 CC]` ✔ · links **D-082** (the ear — Ch 19), **D-079** (crest — Ch 18) · note h (book files these under melanocyte "function" but explains them by migration)
- **Vitiligo** — melanocytes are **lost** later in life (autoimmune), with patchy loss of pigment; linked with other autoimmune disease, especially thyroid. `[E21.01 CC]` ✔ (not a developmental defect — cards say so)

**The appendages (hair, glands, teeth).**
- **Hypertrichosis** — too many hair follicles; a hairy patch over the **lower back** can mark a hidden **spina bifida occulta**. `[E21.02 CC]` ✔ · links Ch 18 (neural tube defects, D-079)
- **Atrichia** — no hair from birth, usually with faults in **other ectodermal derivatives** (teeth, nails) → the **ectodermal dysplasias**. `[E21.02 CC · R05lb]` ✔✔ hair + teeth faults together (XLHED: hypohidrosis — reduced sweating — sparse hair, teeth abnormalities; the abstract does not mention nails) · ✚ infants risk dangerous overheating (R05lb)

**The milk line.**
- **Polythelia** — extra nipples from bits of the **mammary line** that persisted; they can occur **anywhere along the line**. Book: usually in the **axilla** (note i). `[E21.05 CC · R05kw]` ✔ book · ✚ 4.3 % of newborns (R05kw) · ≈ the usual site (no Tier 1 site count retrieved; note i)
- **Polymastia** — a line remnant grows into a **whole breast**. `[E21.05 CC]` ✔
- **Inverted nipple** — the ducts open into the original **pit**, which never rose. `[E21.05 CC]` ✔
- **Problem 1** (extra nipples in the axilla and on the abdomen, both sides) → remnants of the two **mammary lines**, which run from the armpit to the groin. `[E21.07]` ✔ · ✚ two studies found no kidney link for extra nipples (R05kw, R05kx)
- **Gynecomastia, amastia, neonatal breast swelling** — not in Ch 21; not searched to the end (§10).

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Skin colour cells grow from the epidermis."** (map row) **Melanocytes** come from the **neural crest** and move into the epidermis in the first 3 months `[E21.01 · R05ks]` ✔✔.
2. **"The dermis comes from one place."** (map row) Three sources: **lateral plate** (limbs, body wall), **somite dermatomes** (back), **neural crest** (face and neck) `[E21.01]` ✔.
3. **"The periderm is the first horny layer."** It is a **temporary** coat, shed into the amniotic fluid; the horny layer forms later underneath `[E21.01 · R05ku]` ✔✔.
4. **"Hair grows out of the skin's surface."** A hair bud grows **down** into the dermis and wraps round a papilla; the shaft is then pushed up `[E21.02]` ✔.
5. **"Lanugo is the baby's permanent hair."** It is shed about birth and replaced from **new follicles** `[E21.02]` ✔.
6. **"Vernix is just dead skin."** It is **sebaceous secretions plus shed cells and hairs**, and it **protects** skin from soaking `[E21.01]` ✔.
7. **"Nails start on the back of the finger."** Nail fields start at the **tips** and move to the back `[E21.03]` ✔.
8. **"All sweat glands are the same."** **Eccrine** open on the skin and cool; **apocrine** open into hair follicles, in hairy areas `[E21.04]` ✔ · ≈ when apocrine form (note a).
9. **"The breast is a fat organ that grows at puberty."** It is a **modified sweat gland** whose ducts form **before birth**; puberty adds alveoli `[E21.05]` ✔.
10. **"Extra nipples only appear on the chest."** They can be **anywhere on the milk line**, armpit to groin `[E21.05 CC]` ✔.
11. **"An extra nipple means a kidney problem."** Two studies found **no** link `[— · R05kw, R05kx]` ✚ (More only, beyond Langman).
12. **"Albinism means no melanocytes."** Melanocytes are **present**; they cannot make melanin `[E21.01 CC]` ✔.
13. **"Vitiligo is a birth defect."** It is a **later loss** of melanocytes (autoimmune) `[E21.01 CC]` ✔.
14. **"Waardenburg deafness is an ear-bone defect."** It comes from **missing crest melanocytes** in the cochlea `[E21.01 CC]` ✔ · links D-082.
15. **"A hairy patch on the back is harmless."** Over the lower spine it can mark **spina bifida occulta** `[E21.02 CC]` ✔.
16. **"Ichthyosis is always rare and severe."** The commonest form, the filaggrin type, affects ~1 in 250; one faulty copy gives only a very mild form; harlequin is the rare severe end `[— · R05le, R05lf]` ✚ (More only, beyond Langman) · ⚑ b.

## 9. Why learners struggle here (M05)
- **Two sources, then a third** — teach **ectoderm on top, mesoderm below, crest cells moving in** before any layer name; one colour per source.
- **Down-growths** — hair, sebaceous, sweat and mammary glands are all **epidermis growing down**; one animation shows the shared move, then the differences.
- **The milk line** — one line explains both the breast and every extra nipple's position (Problem 1).
- **Crest everywhere** — pigment, face dermis, deafness in WS: link back to Ch 18 (crest), Ch 19 (ear) and Ch 17 (face) instead of new facts.
- **Clocks:** book ages are after fertilization; every fetal skin paper uses an undefined EGA (note f).
