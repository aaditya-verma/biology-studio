## 2. Tier A facts — cited and marked
### E01.03.01 Induction (A, core) · no AN code (§7)
- Organs form when one group of cells (the **inducer**) sends a signal that changes the fate of another group (the **responder**). `[E01.03]` **✔**
- A responder must be ready to answer — its **competence** — and that readiness is itself switched on by a **competence factor**. `[E01.03]` **✔**
- **E01.03.02** · Most inductions happen between an epithelium (cells joined in sheets or tubes) and mesenchyme (loose, scattered cells in matrix). `[E01.03]` **✔**
- **E01.03.02** · Examples: gut endoderm + its mesenchyme → liver and pancreas; limb mesenchyme + overlying ectoderm → limb outgrowth; ureteric bud + metanephric mesenchyme → nephrons. `[E01.03]` **✔** (each is checked in its own chapter: Ch 12, 15, 16)
- Kidney example in detail: the bud makes the mesenchyme form nephrons, and the mesenchyme makes the bud branch — the two induce each other, over and over, building the kidney's shape. `[E01.03 · R05nk]` **✔✔** (*mouse genetics*, review)
- Induction can also run between two epithelia — the book's example is the lens, induced by the optic cup's epithelium. `[E01.03]` **✔** — *cross-chapter note:* Ch 20 teaches the lens placode thickening as the optic **vesicle** touches the surface (`[E20.01]`, ch20 dossier). A Tier 1 review names the optic cup–ectoderm interaction (R05oa, *mouse*), so the book line holds; a card says "the growing optic vesicle/cup" — note h.
- A first signal starts the event, but signals must keep passing **both ways** for the tissues to finish differentiating. `[E01.03 · R05nk]` **✔✔** (reciprocal + repeated, *mouse*) — refutes the map misconception "one-way, one-off".

## 6. Top student misconceptions (refute in cards and Checks — craft §3.4)
1. **"Induction is a one-way, one-off signal."** Signals pass both ways and repeat (kidney) `[E01.03 · R05nk]` ✔✔.
2. **"Imprinting changes the DNA sequence."** It is a methylation mark on one parent's copy; the sequence is the same `[E01.01 · R05nf]` ✔✔.
3. **"Introns are never transcribed."** They are copied into pre-mRNA, then spliced out — never *translated* `[E01.02]` ✔.
4. **"One gene makes one protein."** Splicing and modification make many `[E01.00, E01.02]` ✔.
5. **"Different cells have different genes."** Same genome; different genes are on or off (methylation keeps them so) `[E01.01]` ✔.
6. **"Euchromatin has no nucleosomes."** It is nucleosomal, just looser (⚑ b) `[R05nc]`.
7. **"SHH switches Smoothened on directly."** It removes Patched's brake — a double negative `[E01.05 · R05ny]` ✔✔ (*mouse cells*).
8. **"Hedgehog = SHH."** There are three (desert, Indian, sonic) `[E01.04]` ✔.
9. **"Paracrine = hormones in the blood."** Paracrine is short-range diffusion between neighbours `[E01.04]` ✔ ("blood-borne = endocrine" is general physiology, beyond Langman — More only).
10. **"Juxtacrine signals need no receptor."** They do — on the touching cell, or integrins for matrix; gap junctions are the receptor-free case `[E01.04]` ✔.
11. **"Notch works through second messengers like other receptors."** The cut inside piece goes straight to the nucleus `[E01.05 · R05nr]` ✔✔.
12. **"Knock out a signal gene → always a defect."** Redundancy often covers it `[E01.04]` ✔.
13. **"A morphogen is any signal."** It acts by concentration — different amounts, different fates `[E01.05]` ✔.
14. **"The ligand decides everything."** The receptor matters as much `[E01.04]` ✔.
15. **"An enhancer must sit next to its gene."** It can be far along the DNA `[E01.01]` ✔.
16. **"X inactivation deletes an X."** The X stays; its genes are silenced (⚑ c on how) `[E01.01 · R05nd]`.
